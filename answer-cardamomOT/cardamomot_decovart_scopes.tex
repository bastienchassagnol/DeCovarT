\begin{abstract}
\noindent DeCovarT \parencite{chassagnolDeCovarTMultidimensionalProbalistic2023} estimates cell-type
fractions from bulk \gls{rnaseq}, and models each type by a mean and a gene--gene covariance. CardamomOT
\parencite{maugeCardamomOTMechanisticOptimal2026} infers a signed, directed \gls{grn}
from time-stamped single-cell snapshots, through a mechanistic bursting model. Both pick a small gene
panel from a whole transcriptome, and both estimate a gene--gene structure, but for opposite reasons: in
DeCovarT the covariance is a nuisance that weights genes, in CardamomOT the network is the estimand. We
compare the two along five axes: (i)~gene selection, (ii)~expression versus measurement model, (iii)~a
speculative side axis (convolution, Wiener filter, mean-field ansatz), (iv)~the diagonal of a network and
self-regulation, (v)~how many genes the data can support. On axis (v), DeCovarT needs a covariance,
which shrinkage provides from two cells per type; reading a network requires the support of the
precision, for which the number of cells per type must grow with the square of the largest number of
neighbours of a gene. The code of CardamomOT~2.0.0 (repository state of 26 October 2026) adds a
per-cell depth layer that the paper does not describe, and its diagnostic is the main item DeCovarT could
borrow. CardamomOT could borrow a held-out likelihood for its penalty and a within-basin covariance. An appendix sketches a purely statistical counterpart: a mixture of dynamic Gaussian Bayesian
networks, in which time carries self-regulation and feedback loops.
\end{abstract}

The two models answer different questions (\cref{tab:side}). DeCovarT is a multivariate regression in
the strict sense: several correlated responses (the genes) share one design (the fractions). Its gene
covariance matters because it weights genes, as in \gls{gls}. CardamomOT is a mechanistic model of the
cell, and its network is what the user reads. A good covariance for DeCovarT need not be a good network
for CardamomOT, and the converse also holds (\cref{sec:diag}).

\section{Derivation of regularised covariance or regulatory networks(axes i and v)}\label{sec:genes}

\subsection{CardamomOT}

The paper treats the panel as prior information. \enquote{All experimental applications presented here
operate on gene subsets rather than the full transcriptome (Appendix C), which is not a neutral choice}
(p.~27); \enquote{the selected gene set should be recognised as a form of prior information in its own
right} (p.~27). It recommends a core set of nodes from prior knowledge, completed by curated interaction
databases and a coarse genome-wide network, and calls the automation \enquote{ongoing work} (p.~28). For
Semrau and Kameneva, the applications use gene lists from earlier work; for Schiebinger, markers plus
\enquote{dynamically variable genes} ranked by entropy and Wasserstein distances (p.~32).

The code of CardamomOT~2.0.0 automates that recommendation (module docstring of
\cmfile{inference/gene_selection.py}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{gene universe:} mitochondrial, ribosomal-protein and non-coding genes are excluded by name
        or biotype;
  \item \textbf{terminal genes} (\cmfile{inference/gandrillon.py}), from Gandrillon's workflow.
        \enquote{KD} is the one-dimensional \glslink{def-wasserstein}{Wasserstein distance} between the
        counts of a gene at two consecutive time points, divided by the gene's standard deviation over
        the pair. \enquote{MDE} is the absolute change of a discrete
        \glslink{def-entropy}{Shannon entropy} estimated by the \gls{bub} estimator \parencite{paninskiEstimationEntropyMutual2003}. Terminals are then chosen by a round robin over cell
        types, each tested against the rest by a Wilcoxon test on log-normalised counts (Scanpy's
        \cmfile{rank_genes_groups}) \parencite{wolfSCANPYLargescaleSinglecell2018};
  \item \textbf{basin separability} (\cmfile{nb_init_separation}): genes whose negative-binomial modes
        are well separated;
  \item \textbf{a coarse network} on highly variable genes (OTVelo-Corr);
  \item \textbf{closure:} a budgeted prize-collecting Steiner selection that anchors the terminals to the
        stimuli.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Gandrillon's criteria.} The \enquote{Kantorovich distance} cited by Gandrillon (p.~7, to Vershik
2006) is the Wasserstein-1 distance $W_1$, and the code computes it with
\cmfile{scipy.stats.wasserstein_distance}.

\subsection{DeCovarT}\label{sec:decovart-genes}

The estimand is a gene panel that makes $(\bmu_j,\SW)$ estimable and informative about $\bm p$, not
population-level differential expression. With one specimen, cells are not replicates, so a $p$-value
describes a screen inside that specimen \parencite{zimmermanPracticalSolutionPseudoreplication2021,
squairConfrontingFalseDiscoveries2021}. The tiers are written in the
\vignette{03-1-feature-selection.html}{feature-selection vignette}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{detection:} genes detected in too few reference cells are removed;
  \item \textbf{presence in every type}, so that no within-type variance $V_{gg}$ is zero: necessary for a
        non-singular estimate of $\SW$, not sufficient (\cref{sec:ceiling}); this also removes perfect
        markers, for which proportions are not identified once gene-wise platform ratios are allowed
        \parencite[p.~7]{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026};
  \item \textbf{consistency screen} (\enquote{marker gene consistency},
        \textcite[p.~2]{wangBulkTissueCell2019}): the between/within ratio on
        \glslink{def-pearson}{Pearson residuals}, or its count-scale versions, the analysis-of-deviance
        and \glslink{def-qlf}{quasi-likelihood $F$ tests} \parencite{lundDetectingDifferentialExpression2012};
        the test is a gate, and genes are ranked by effect size;
  \item \textbf{optional refinement} by the \glslink{def-fisher}{Fisher information for $\bm p$}, which
        needs $\SW$.
\end{itemize}
For two types and one gene, under the single-cell model with equal within-type variances $\sigma_g^2$,
the Fisher information for $p$ is $n_i\Delta_g^2/\sigma_g^2$, with $\Delta_g=\mu_{g1}-\mu_{g2}$
(\cref{app:fisher}) \chk{V4a}. The \gls{anova} ratio on $C_1=C_2=C$ cells per type is the plug-in
estimate of $(C/2)\Delta_g^2/\sigma_g^2$, so both rank genes alike; its mean is close to
$1+(C/2)\Delta_g^2/\sigma_g^2$ \chk{V4b}. With unequal variances or correlated genes they do not: the
multivariate information with a common $\bSigma_W$ is $n_i\bm\Delta\T\bSigma_W^{-1}\bm\Delta$, a squared
\glslink{def-mahalanobis}{Mahalanobis distance} \chk{V4d}.
With a common $\bSigma_W$, the squared \glslink{def-hellinger}{Hellinger distance} between the two type
laws is $1-\exp(-\tfrac18\bm\Delta\T\bSigma_W^{-1}\bm\Delta)$, increasing in that distance \chk{V8a}; the
2-Wasserstein distance is not, since for equal covariances it reduces to $\lVert\bm\Delta\rVert$
\chk{V8b}, and the two can rank candidate panels in opposite orders \chk{V8c}. This links the screen to the
overlap criteria of the \vignette{S6-feature-screening-scenario.html}{screening scenario}. The one-gene view
is the screen, not the objective: with type-specific covariances, both the Fisher information for $p$ and the
overlap of the two Gaussian type laws add a covariance-difference term to the Mahalanobis separation of the
means (\cref{app:fisher}, \cref{eq:fisher-multi,eq:bhattacharyya}).

The two methods push the within-group variance in opposite directions. CardamomOT keeps genes whose
distribution changes in time and splits into separable basins, so multimodality is wanted. DeCovarT
wants a unimodal law within each type, with a covariance it can estimate.

\subsection{How many genes can the data support?}\label{sec:ceiling}

The answer depends on what is estimated from the $C_j$ cells of each type. The box below separates the
two goals; the symbols are those of \cref{tab:notation}.

\begin{idbox}[title={Identifiability: covariance versus network}]
\textbf{(a) The covariance $\SW$: DeCovarT's plug-in.} $\SW$ holds every association between two genes,
direct or through other genes. DeCovarT's likelihood needs a positive-definite estimate close to $\SW$
in a covariance loss, such as the expected Gaussian log-score of \cref{sec:borrow} or a Frobenius norm.
Whether the zeros of $\hat\bOmega_j$ are the true ones does not enter. What matters is that the estimate
is well defined (finite, positive definite and unique), not that some algorithm returns a matrix.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The unconstrained \gls{mle}.} With the mean estimated, it is the empirical
        covariance $\bm V_j=\bm X_c\T\bm X_c/C_j$, where $\bm X_c$ is the centred $C_j\times G$ matrix of the
        type-$j$ cells. Its rank is that of $\bm X_c$, at most $\min(C_j-1,G)$, with equality for data in
        general position, which holds almost surely for Gaussian cells: $C_j\bm V_j\sim W_G(C_j-1,\SW)$
        \parencite[ch.~3]{muirheadAspectsMultivariateStatistical1982} \chk{V10a}. $\bm V_j$ is
        positive definite if and only if $\operatorname{rank}\bm X_c=G$. This needs $C_j\ge G+1$
        \emph{and} no exact linear or affine relation between genes; such a relation lowers the rank
        further \chk{V10b}, and with $C_j\le G$ the centred profiles always satisfy one. When $\bm V_j$ is
        singular, the Gaussian log-likelihood is unbounded above along its null directions, and the
        \glsxtrshort{mle} does not exist \chk{V10c}.
  \item \textbf{The presence gate is necessary, not sufficient.} A gene constant within type $j$ has
        $V_{gg}=0$, the simplest exact relation; the gate of \cref{sec:decovart-genes} removes it, and it
        keeps the diagonal shrinkage target of \cref{tab:covexist} positive definite. With $C_j\le G$, or another exact relation,
        $\bm V_j$ stays singular after the gate \chk{V10b}.
  \item \textbf{Relaxing the requirement} (\cref{tab:covexist}). The penalty, the constraint or the
        structure, not the data, makes the estimate exist when $C_j\le G$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Shrinkage} \parencite{schäferShrinkageApproachLargescale2005}
        \parencite{ledoitWellconditionedEstimatorLargedimensional2004}\footnote{Overview:
        \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimation_of_covariance_matrices\#Shrinkage_estimation}{Wikipedia,

        estimated intensity $\hat\rho$ is truncated to $[0,1]$, and $\hat\rho=0$ returns $\bm V_j$. The
        diagonal target needs every $V_{gg}>0$, hence the presence gate; the scaled identity needs only
        $\bm V_j\neq\bm 0$.
  \item \textbf{Condition number} \parencite{wonConditionnumberregularizedCovarianceEstimation2013}: they maximise the Gaussian log-likelihood under
        $\operatorname{cond}(\bSigma)\le\kappa_{\max}$ (eq.~6, p.~4). The solution keeps the eigenvectors of
        $\bm V_j$ and replaces each eigenvalue $l_g$ by $\min\{\max(\tau^\star,l_g),\kappa_{\max}\tau^\star\}$
        (eq.~7, p.~5), with $\tau^\star$ in closed form and found in $O(G)$ operations (eqs~11--12, p.~7). The
        solution is unique when $\kappa_{\max}\le\operatorname{cond}(\bm V_j)$ (Theorem~1, pp.~6--7), which
        always holds for a singular $\bm V_j$, and it is well conditioned \enquote{even when $n<p$} (p.~5).
        $\kappa_{\max}$ is chosen by predictive likelihood (Section~3, p.~9). Their model has a known zero
        mean; with an estimated mean we read the condition as $\bm V_j\neq\bm 0$, i.e.\ $C_j\ge2$ with one
        non-constant gene. No sparsity is assumed.
  \item \textbf{Structure on $\bSigma$.} A factor, low-rank-plus-diagonal, banded or linear model has fewer
        parameters than $G(G+1)/2$. \parencite{zwiernikMaximumLikelihoodEstimation2017} study the linear models $\bSigma_v=\bm G_0+\sum_kv_k\bm G_k$, which include
        correlation matrices and prescribed zeros in $\bSigma$ (pp.~1269--1270). The likelihood is not
        concave there; it is strictly concave on $\{\bSigma:\bm 0\prec\bSigma\prec2\bm V_j\}$ (their
        Proposition~1, p.~1273), and for $C_j\gtrsim14G$ hill climbing from the least-squares estimate reaches
        the global maximum with high probability (p.~1269). Their existence result assumes a full-rank sample
        covariance (Proposition~5, p.~1278), so it does not relax $C_j\ge G+1$; their \enquote{with probability
        1 when $n\geqslant p$} should read $n\ge p+1$ when the mean is estimated \chk{V10d}. Below full rank,
        existence depends on the structure; for a factor model a variance $\psi_g\to0$ (a Heywood case) can
        make the likelihood unbounded (our reading, not checked).
  \item \textbf{Graphical lasso}: with $\lambda>0$, positive $V_{gg}$ and an unpenalised diagonal, it has a
        unique positive-definite solution for any $C_j\ge2$, even when $C_j<G$
        \parencite{carterExistencePenalisedLikelihood2025}. The penalty, not the data, makes the estimate
        exist below $C_j=G+1$.
\end{itemize}
None of these estimators needs a sparse truth.

\medskip
\textbf{(b) The support of $\bOmega_j$: the network.} This is CardamomOT's kind of estimand: its
$\bm\theta$ is directed, but it is read through its non-zero entries. On the graph $\mathcal G$ of
$\bOmega_j$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \emph{clique} is a set of genes pairwise joined by edges; the clique number $\cl(\mathcal G)$
        is the size of the largest clique;
  \item a graph is \emph{chordal} if every cycle through four or more genes has a chord, an edge between
        two genes that are not consecutive on the cycle. The treewidth plus one, $\tw(\mathcal G)+1$, is
        \enquote{the clique number of a minimal chordal cover}, the smallest clique number of a chordal
        graph that contains $\mathcal G$ \parencite{bernsteinMaximumLikelihoodThresholds2023} (p.~2);
  \item the degree $\deg(g)$ counts the neighbours of gene $g$, and $\degmax$ is its maximum over genes.
\end{itemize}
The requirements, from the weakest to the strongest:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Existence} of the penalised estimate, as in (a). The graphical lasso estimator
        \enquote{always exists} (Bernstein \& Outlaw, p.~2); its convergence says nothing about the graph.
  \item \textbf{Existence of the \gls{mle} on a fixed graph}, i.e.\ of the refit under the selected
        zeros. For a Gaussian model $\mathcal M$ with mean zero, \enquote{the minimum $n$ such that the MLE
        exists for almost every $X\in\mathbb R^{p\times n}$ is called the maximum likelihood threshold
        (MLT) of $\mathcal M$}, and the model of a graph with edge set $E$ is
        $\mathcal M_{\mathcal G}=\{K\succ0:K_{ij}=0\text{ whenever }ij\notin E\}$ (Bernstein \& Outlaw,
        p.~2); their $n$ and $p$ are our $C_j$ and $G$, and we write $\mlt(\mathcal G)$ for the
        \gls{mltabbr}. They state these bounds:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item $\mlt(\mathcal G)\le G$, from Dempster (p.~2);
          \item $\cl(\mathcal G)\le\mlt(\mathcal G)\le\tw(\mathcal G)+1$, from Buhl (p.~2); a chordal
                graph is its own minimal chordal cover, so $\mlt=\cl$ for chordal graphs
                \parencite{uhlerGeometryMaximumLikelihood2012,grossMaximumLikelihoodThreshold2018};
          \item $\mlt(\mathcal G)$ is at most the generic completion rank of $\mathcal G$, with equality
                for nine or fewer vertices (their Theorem~2.2, p.~3);
          \item the $k$-core of $\mathcal G$ is what remains after repeatedly deleting genes of degree
                less than $k$, and the smallest $k$ with an empty $k$-core bounds $\mlt(\mathcal G)$ from
                above (p.~5, after Gross \& Sullivant); sparse graphs have small bounds.
        \end{itemize}
        Their model has a known zero mean. With the mean estimated from the $C_j$ cells, the centred
        sample has rank $C_j-1$, so the requirement reads $C_j-1\ge\mlt(\mathcal G)$ (our reading). In
        their simulations (at most nine variables, independent Gaussian data), the probability that the
        graph selected by the graphical lasso has an \glsxtrshort{mltabbr} within the sample size rises
        with the penalty and with the sample size; with one observation, the graphical lasso
        \enquote{never selected the graphical model with no edges, the only model with MLT 1}
        (pp.~4--5).
  \item \textbf{Support recovery}: $C_j\gtrsim\degmax^2\log G$ for the graphical lasso, against
        $C_j\gtrsim\degmax\log G$ for neighbourhood selection
        \parencite[p.~41]{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015}, under incoherence (irrepresentability),
        bounded eigenvalues and a minimum $\lvert\omega_{gh}\rvert$ \parencite{ravikumarHighdimensionalCovarianceEstimation2011}. These conditions involve the unknown $\SW$, and \enquote{we can never verify
        them on real data} \parencite[p.~77]{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015}. With $G=500$ and
        $\degmax=10$, $\degmax^2\log G\approx620$ before constants, and hubs raise $\degmax$.
\end{enumerate}
DeCovarT's plug-in needs (a) only. Reading $\hat\bOmega_j$, or CardamomOT's $\hat{\bm\theta}$, as a
network needs (b).
\end{idbox}

CardamomOT has $G^2$ interaction parameters (\enquote{$10^4$ for $n=100$}, p.~27, where $n$ is our $G$)
and recommends \enquote{not exceeding of the order of 100 genes in the fully unconstrained setting}
(p.~28), with more genes allowed when $\bm\theta_0$ fixes more entries. DeCovarT faces the same trade-off
per type: several Suppinger types have $C_j\le G$, which rules out the unpenalised covariance and puts
support recovery out of reach for all but very sparse graphs. Its plug-in only needs goal (a), which is
why dense shrinkage \parencite{schäferShrinkageApproachLargescale2005} is the default.

\textbf{Selection removes what it should estimate.} A panel chosen for small within-type variance, such
as ReDeconv's smallest \glspl{cv} \parencite[p.~14]{luTranscriptomeSizeMatters2025} or MuSiC's
\enquote{cross-cell consistent genes} \parencite[p.~3]{wangBulkTissueCell2019}, leaves covariances
dominated by Poisson sampling. Their correlations are then attenuated (\cref{sec:noise}), and the
estimated precision looks diagonal for reasons that have nothing to do with regulation.

\subsection{Penalties across gene pairs and across classes}\label{sec:joint}

\textbf{Prior structure.} An edge-weighted graphical lasso takes a penalty matrix
$\bm\Lambda=(\lambda_{gh})$: small weights for whitelisted pairs, zero constraints for blacklisted ones
\parencite{friedmanSparseInverseCovariance2008}. CardamomOT's prior network, or an OmniPath prior as in
its repository (p.~27), can enter DeCovarT this way. Neighbourhood selection fits $G$ lasso regressions
\parencite{meinshausenHighdimensionalGraphsVariable2006}; it scales well but returns a
graph, not a positive-definite $\hat\bSigma$, so a refit under the selected zeros is needed, which is
requirement (b)2 of \cref{sec:ceiling}. A penalty chosen for prediction does not select the true
neighbourhood (their Proposition~1, p.~5), which matters for CardamomOT, not for DeCovarT's plug-in.

\textbf{Several classes at once.} DeCovarT estimates each $\SW$ from the cells of type $j$ alone. Joint
estimation couples $K$ precision matrices $\bOmega^{(1)},\dots,\bOmega^{(K)}$, whose classes can be cell
types, time points or batches. The methods differ in what the classes share.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The support only.} The graphical group lasso of
        \textcite{chiquetInferringMultipleGraphical2011} groups the $K$ partial correlations of each gene
        pair, so that edges are \enquote{present or absent together across the conditions}, \enquote{not
        necessarily with the same strength}; an edge present in one class and absent in another then has
        probability zero \parencite[p.~51]{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015}. The cooperative lasso
        splits each group into its positive and negative parts: an edge may vanish in some classes, but
        it keeps one sign where it is present, since \enquote{sign swaps are very unlikely} (p.~51). The
        \gls{ggl} in the box below is of the same kind.
  \item \textbf{The weights as well.} The \gls{fgl} \parencite{danaherJointGraphicalLasso2012} also
        penalises differences between edge values (box below). PLNFGL
        \parencite{zhaiPLNFGLJointEstimation2026} estimates each class covariance under a \gls{pln} model
        by moments and embeds it into the fused graphical lasso (their eq.~6, p.~3); its authors note that
        this step \enquote{may face computational challenges when applied to large gene sets or to
        datasets involving more than five conditions} (p.~10). The \gls{jgcgm} \parencite{wuJointLearningMultiple2020} assumes a nonparanormal law (a Gaussian copula) in each class and
        writes each precision as a common part plus a class-specific part,
        $\bOmega^{(k)}=\bm M+\bm H^{(k)}$, with $\ell_1$ penalties weighted by $aK$ on $\bm M$ and by
        $1-a$ on each $\bm H^{(k)}$, $0<a<1$ (p.~4).
  \item \textbf{The difference only.} For two classes, the lasso-penalised D-trace loss
        \parencite{yuanDifferentialNetworkAnalysis2017} estimates $\bOmega^{(2)}-\bOmega^{(1)}$
        \enquote{without attempting to estimate precision matrices} (p.~2): the loss has its unique
        minimiser at the difference of the two inverse covariances (p.~5). Only the difference needs to be
        sparse.
\end{itemize}
These penalties serve goal (b). For DeCovarT's plug-in, a joint penalty helps only if it lowers the
covariance loss of every type, and the weighting caveat in the box below makes that doubtful for rare
types (our reading, not tested).

\begin{methodbox}[title={Joint graphical lasso: group versus fused penalty}]
\textcite{danaherJointGraphicalLasso2012} estimate $K$ precision matrices from classes of $C_k$ cells with
empirical covariances $\bm V^{(k)}$. In our notation, they maximise (their eq.~2.4, p.~3)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jgl}
\sum_{k=1}^KC_k\bigl\{\log\det\bOmega^{(k)}-\tr\bigl(\bm V^{(k)}\bOmega^{(k)}\bigr)\bigr\}
-P\bigl(\bOmega^{(1)},\dots,\bOmega^{(K)}\bigr)
\end{equation}
over positive-definite $\bOmega^{(1)},\dots,\bOmega^{(K)}$, with one of two penalties (eqs~2.5 and 2.6,
p.~4):
\begin{align}
P_{\mathrm{FGL}}&=\lambda_1\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{g\neq h}\bigl\lvert\omega^{(k)}_{gh}\bigr\rvert
  +\lambda_2\sum_{k<k'}\sum_{g,h}\bigl\lvert\omega^{(k)}_{gh}-\omega^{(k')}_{gh}\bigr\rvert,
  \label{eq:fgl}\\
P_{\mathrm{GGL}}&=\lambda_1\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{g\neq h}\bigl\lvert\omega^{(k)}_{gh}\bigr\rvert
  +\lambda_2\sum_{g\neq h}\Bigl\{\sum_{k=1}^K\bigl(\omega^{(k)}_{gh}\bigr)^2\Bigr\}^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:ggl}
\end{align}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fused versus group.} A large $\lambda_2$ in \eqref{eq:fgl} makes many entries identical
        across classes. With $\lambda_1=0$ and $\lambda_2>0$, \eqref{eq:ggl} gives every
        $\hat\bOmega^{(k)}$ the same pattern of non-zero entries. \enquote{GGL encourages a weaker form of
        similarity across the K precision matrices than does FGL: the latter encourages shared edge values
        across the K matrices, whereas the former encourages only a shared pattern of sparsity} (p.~4).
  \item \textbf{Classes.} Cell types, time points or batches. For one cell type followed over ordered
        time points $k=1,\dots,K$, the fused sum can be restricted to consecutive times,
        $\lambda_2\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\sum_{g,h}\lvert\omega^{(k+1)}_{gh}-\omega^{(k)}_{gh}\rvert$: the
        network may drift, but each change between neighbouring times is penalised. This is our
        adaptation; \eqref{eq:fgl} fuses every pair of classes, and Danaher et al.\ point to time-varying
        network methods for ordered classes (p.~8).
  \item \textbf{Weighting caveat.} In \eqref{eq:jgl}, \enquote{the contribution of each class to the
        penalized log likelihood is weighted by its size; consequently, the largest class can have outsize
        influence on the estimated networks} (p.~13); in their lung application they weighted the classes
        equally (p.~12). \textcite[p.~49]{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015} sets the same condition
        for one penalty shared by all classes: it is sensible \enquote{provided that the C samples have
        similar sizes}. The \glsxtrshort{jgcgm} objective is weighted by the class sizes in the same way
        (eq.~2, p.~3). With Suppinger's types, the edges of a rare type with a small $C_k$ would be fused
        towards, or grouped with, those of the abundant types, and edges specific to the rare type could
        be set to zero.
\end{itemize}
\end{methodbox}

\subsection{CardamomOT's \texorpdfstring{$\ell_1$}{l1} penalty: its level, an error-rate reading, alternatives}\label{sec:lambda}

\textbf{Paper.} Step~2 of the inference updates the network by (Eq.~10, p.~9)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cardamom-l1}
\hat{\bm\theta}=\arg\min_{\bm\theta}\sum_c\operatorname{loss}\bigl\{k^{\bm\theta}_{\mathrm{on}}(\bm P_c),
\bm k_{z_c}\bigr\}+\lambda\lVert\bm\theta-\bm\theta_0\rVert_1,
\end{equation}
a cross-entropy between the predicted burst rates and the basin modes, summed over the cells $c$. The paper
sets \enquote{$\lambda=0.05\cdot N_c/\sqrt{N_g}$, where $N_c$ is the number of cells, reflecting the
heuristic that the number of active regulators per gene scales at most as $\sqrt{N_g}$, and that a per-gene
basin label attribution error of 5\,\% is a reasonable target for reliable inference} (p.~9); $N_g$ is our
$G$.

\textbf{Code (version 2.0.0).} This formula does not appear. \cmfile{inference/network.py} fits one target
gene $g$ at a time, with one level $\lambda_g$ for the row $\bm\theta_{g\cdot}$. The function
\cmfile{qut_penalty} returns the \gls{qut} \parencite{giacobinoQuantileUniversalThreshold2017}: the \cmfile{level}-quantile of $\max_h\lvert\partial L/\partial
\theta_{gh}\rvert$ at the null fit (no interaction, per-sample basal parameters fitted), over permutations
of the target within each sample. The pipeline passes \cmfile{qut_level=0.5} and \cmfile{qut_n_perm=20}
(\cmfile{model/base.py}), which override the function's own default level of 0.95. The adaptive-lasso
refinement has its own threshold at the same level (\cmfile{qut_garrote}). The $\ell_1$ norm is smoothed,
quadratic for $\lvert x\rvert<10^{-3}$.

\begin{methodbox}[title={Where the two levels come from, and what they control (our reading)}]
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The paper's $\lambda$ as a loss budget.} For one target gene, $\sqrt G$ edges of unit size
        $\lvert\theta_{gh}-\theta_{0,gh}\rvert=1$ cost $\lambda\sqrt G=0.05N_c$ \chk{V12d}: the loss of
        mislabelling 5\,\% of the $N_c$ cells if a wrong basin costs about one unit of cross-entropy. Edges
        enter only if they buy more than that. The paper gives no derivation; this is our reconstruction.
  \item \textbf{Its scale.} $\lambda/N_c=0.05/\sqrt G$ is fixed, so $\lambda$ is a floor on the mean
        per-cell score of an edge, not a significance level. The null score, a sum of $N_c$ independent
        per-cell terms, has a standard deviation of order $\sqrt{N_c}$, so $\lambda$ over the noise grows as
        $\sqrt{N_c}$ \chk{V12d}: few false edges in large data sets, but weak true edges never enter.
  \item \textbf{The \glsxtrshort{qut}.} By the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions, the fit of target $g$ has no
        edge if and only if $\lambda_g\ge\max_h\lvert s_h\rvert$, with $s_h=\partial L/\partial\theta_{gh}$ at
        the null fit. $\lambda_{\mathrm{QUT}}$ is the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $\max_h\lvert s_h\rvert$ under the
        null: the smallest $\lambda$ that returns the empty model with probability $1-\alpha$. The code takes
        the null by permutation and $\alpha=1-\texttt{qut\_level}=0.5$, estimated from 20 permutations.
\end{itemize}
Assumptions for the next items: target $g$ has $R$ candidate regulators, $R_1$ true and $R_0=R-R_1$ null;
the null scores are independent and Gaussian (orthogonal design) and do not depend on the true edges; the
true scores lie far above the threshold. The ledger writes $m$ for $R$ and $k$ for $R_1$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\gls{fwer}.} $\lambda_{\mathrm{QUT}}$ controls the probability of at least one false edge
        into $g$ at $\alpha$ under the global null of $g$. With $R_1$ true regulators, the per-target
        \glsxtrshort{fwer} is $1-(1-\alpha)^{R_0/R}\le\alpha$ \chk{V12c}. The control is per target gene: over
        $G$ independent fits, the network-wide \glsxtrshort{fwer} is close to 1 at $\alpha=0.5$.
  \item \textbf{Bonferroni.} By the union bound, $\lambda_{\mathrm{QUT}}$ is never above the Bonferroni
        threshold $\sigma\Phi^{-1}\{1-\alpha/(2R)\}$, whatever the dependence between scores. Under
        independence it is the Šidák threshold, with per-test level $1-(1-\alpha)^{1/R}\ge\alpha/R$
        \chk{V12a}. Both thresholds grow as $\sigma\sqrt{2\log R}$ and their ratio tends to 1 \chk{V12b}: at
        $R=100$ it is 0.998 for $\alpha=0.05$ and 0.962 for $\alpha=0.5$. The per-test levels differ by the
        factor $-\log(1-\alpha)/\alpha$, 1.03 at $\alpha=0.05$ and 1.39 at $\alpha=0.5$ \chk{V12a}.
  \item \textbf{False edges and an \glsxtrshort{fdr}-type level.} The expected number of false edges into
        $g$ tends to $-\log(1-\alpha)$ when $R_1=\sqrt R$ and $R\to\infty$ \chk{V12c}: 0.69 at $\alpha=0.5$,
        about $0.7G$ false edges in the network. With $R=100$ and $R_1=10$ (the paper's $\sqrt G$), the
        false-edge share $\E V/(\E V+R_1)$ is 0.059 at $\alpha=0.5$ and 0.005 at $\alpha=0.05$ \chk{V12c}; a
        ratio of expectations, not the \glsxtrshort{fdr} itself. A share $q$ needs the per-test level
        $t=qR_1/\{(1-q)R_0\}$, about $qR_1/\{(1-q)\alpha\}$ times the Bonferroni level $\alpha/R$ \chk{V12c}:
        an \glsxtrshort{fdr}-type target is about $R_1$ times more permissive than a \glsxtrshort{fwer} one.
  \item \textbf{Dependence.} Regulator proteins are correlated, so the scores are not independent. The
        within-sample permutation keeps that dependence: the \glsxtrshort{qut} still controls the per-target
        \glsxtrshort{fwer} under the permutation null, and stays below the Bonferroni threshold by the union
        bound. The expected counts and shares above assume independence. With 20 permutations the median
        itself is uncertain.
\end{itemize}
\end{methodbox}

\textbf{Alternatives to the element-wise $\ell_1$ on $\bm\theta$.} $\bm\theta$ is directed
($\theta_{gh}\neq\theta_{hg}$) and enters a logistic burst rate, so its likelihood is not a function of a
covariance.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{D-trace loss.} \textcite{yuanDifferentialNetworkAnalysis2017} minimise
        $L_D(\bm\Delta)=\tfrac14(\langle\bSigma_X\bm\Delta,\bm\Delta\bSigma_Y\rangle+\langle\bSigma_Y\bm\Delta,
        \bm\Delta\bSigma_X\rangle)-\langle\bm\Delta,\bSigma_X-\bSigma_Y\rangle$, convex with unique minimiser
        $\bSigma_Y^{-1}-\bSigma_X^{-1}$ (eq.~1, p.~5), a generalisation of the D-trace loss of
        \textcite{zhangSparsePrecisionMatrix2014} (p.~4). It needs a symmetric parameter and second-moment
        matrices as data; neither holds for $\bm\theta$. Two ideas carry over: a loss whose minimiser is the
        target without a log-determinant, and a penalty on a difference only. The code already does the
        second across conditions, $\bm\theta_c=\bm\theta_{\mathrm{shared}}+\bm\delta_c$ with an $\ell_1$ on
        $\bm\delta_c$ (\cmfile{core_inference_conditions}). A directed analogue of the D-trace loss would be a
        squared residual of the Lyapunov equation of \cref{sec:diag}, $\lVert\bm A\bm V_b+\bm V_b\bm A\T+\bm
        Q\rVert_F^2$ per basin $b$, with an $\ell_1$ on the off-diagonal of $\bm A$ (speculative: it rests on
        the linearisation).
  \item \textbf{Log-determinant loss} (graphical lasso). It needs a symmetric $\bOmega\succ0$ and a Gaussian
        likelihood, so it does not apply to $\bm\theta$. Its existence argument does carry over: the
        penalty makes the estimate exist. An unpenalised logistic fit diverges for a separable target, and
        the code bounds those edges with a Gaussian prior (\cmfile{ridge_coefficients}, \enquote{bounds the
        edges of nearly separable targets}). The graphical lasso would fit the within-basin covariance that
        CardamomOT lacks (\cref{sec:borrow}), as an undirected layer next to $\bm\theta$.
  \item \textbf{Group or cooperative lasso.} Chiquet's eqs~2.20--2.21 group the partial correlations of a
        gene pair across conditions; the cooperative version splits each group into positive and negative
        parts, so an edge may vanish in some classes but keeps one sign
        \parencite[p.~51]{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015}
        \parencite{chiquetSparsitySigncoherentGroups2012}. For $\bm\theta$ the classes can be conditions or
        cell types (the code fuses edge values; a group penalty would share only the support), time
        intervals (fused between consecutive times, as in the box of \cref{sec:joint}, or grouped), or the
        $B$ basins of the target. With basin-specific interactions $\bm\theta_{gh}=(\theta^{(1)}_{gh},\dots,
        \theta^{(B)}_{gh})$ and prior weights $w_h$, the cooperative penalty reads
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:coop-basin}
        P(\bm\theta_{g\cdot})=\lambda\sum_{h}w_h\bigl\{\lVert(\bm\theta_{gh}-\theta_{0,gh}\bm 1)_+\rVert_2
        +\lVert(\bm\theta_{gh}-\theta_{0,gh}\bm 1)_-\rVert_2\bigr\},
        \end{equation}
        with $u_+=\max(0,u)$ and $u_-=\max(0,-u)$ entrywise. A regulator then enters for all basins of $g$
        or for none (group lasso, \textcite{yuanModelSelectionEstimation2006}), with one sign across basins
        (cooperative lasso): an activator stays an activator, and the $B$ estimates of one edge share
        strength. The \glsxtrshort{qut} carries over through the dual norm: with $\bm s_h$ the vector of the
        $B$ null scores of edge $h\to g$, it is a quantile of
        $\max_h\max(\lVert\bm s_h^+\rVert_2,\lVert\bm s_h^-\rVert_2)/w_h$ for \eqref{eq:coop-basin}
        (Chiquet, p.~80; our reading). Speculative: CardamomOT's $\bm\theta$ is shared across basins by
        construction (Eq.~1, p.~6), and basin-specific interactions need about $B$ times more cells per edge.
\end{itemize}

\begin{answerbox}[title=In short: gene selection]
\begin{itemize}
  \item CardamomOT selects for change in time and multimodality, and treats the panel as prior
        information. DeCovarT selects for between-type separation with an estimable unimodal within-type
        law. The criteria are not interchangeable.
  \item The univariate \glsxtrshort{anova} ratio is a screen; the Fisher information for $\bm p$ is the
        objective. For two types they agree, for every $p$, only with equal variances \chk{V4c}; summing
        them over genes needs independent genes, except on a measure-zero set of correlations \chk{V4e}.
  \item DeCovarT's plug-in needs a well-defined covariance: the empirical one only from $C_j\ge G+1$ with
        no exact relation between genes \chk{V10a,V10b}, shrinkage for any $C_j\ge2$ \chk{V11b}. A network reading
        needs the support of the precision, with $C_j-1\ge\mlt(\mathcal G)$ for a refit and
        $C_j\gtrsim\degmax^2\log G$ for recovery. Prior structure (a whitelist, $\bm\theta_0$) or a joint
        penalty across classes borrows strength, the latter at the expense of rare types.
  \item CardamomOT's paper sets $\lambda=0.05N_c/\sqrt G$, a per-cell effect floor; its code uses a
        \glsxtrshort{qut} at level 0.5 per target gene, a per-target \glsxtrshort{fwer} of 0.5 that, with
        about $\sqrt G$ regulators per gene, leaves a false-edge share near 6\,\% \chk{V12c}.
\end{itemize}
\end{answerbox}

\section{Expression model versus measurement model (axis ii)}\label{sec:noise}

\textcite[p.~3]{sarkarSeparatingMeasurementExpression2021} separate \enquote{a measurement model
[\ldots] the conditional distribution $p(X\mid\Lambda)$} from \enquote{a model for the expression levels
$p(\Lambda)$}. For \glsxtrshort{umi} data, Poisson measurement suffices: $x_{gc}\mid\lambda_{gc}\sim
\Pois(x_{c+}\lambda_{gc})$, and gene--gene correlation belongs to the expression model.
\textcite{fangExtrinsicBiologicalStochasticity2025} add a cell-wide factor
$c_{\mathrm{tech}}c_{\mathrm{bio}}$, our $s_c$, that multiplies the mean of every gene (their eqs~1--2,
pp.~3--4). For intrinsically uncorrelated genes, the normalised covariance $\Cov(X_a,X_b)/(\E X_a\E X_b)$
then equals the extrinsic noise $\Var s/(\E s)^2$ (eq.~5, p.~5) \chk{V9b}. They estimate it by the mode of
the normalised covariances, call \enquote{Poisson genes} those whose bootstrap interval of normalised
variance contains it, and take the Poisson \glsxtrshort{mle} of the size factor on those genes (p.~16).
Normalising by size factors \enquote{can eliminate biological stochasticity present in extrinsic
\enquote{noise}} (p.~2).

\begin{figure}[tbp]

\textbf{CardamomOT, paper.} The \gls{nb} comes from bursting and is an expression model (p.~6). No size
factor, library size or capture term appears in Eqs~3--4; technical noise is examined only as a
robustness test with simulated binomial dropout of 5--90\,\% (p.~17).

\textbf{CardamomOT, code (version 2.0.0).} The measurement layer is a per-cell depth factor $s_c$
(\cmfile{estimate_cell_depth.py}, \cmfile{inference/depth.py}, \cmfile{inference/depth_model.py}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rationale} (script docstring): a depth common to all genes of a cell \enquote{acts like a
        hidden common regulator: it correlates all genes and can move cells between NB basins}.
  \item \textbf{Estimation} within homogeneous groups (sample, time, cell type), on the whole transcriptome
        (at least 10{,}000 genes, otherwise nothing is estimated); the library size leaves out the 50 most
        expressed genes. The default method, \texttt{poissonian}, follows Fang \& Pachter: extrinsic noise
        as the mode of normalised covariances, iterated Poisson genes, $s_c$ as the Poisson
        \glsxtrshort{mle} on those genes, then a pairwise least-squares rescaling between groups. The
        alternative, \cmfile{group_median}, divides the library size by the group median.
  \item \textbf{How it enters:} with the default \cmfile{depth_model='mean'}, counts are
        $\NB(\kappa,r/s_c)$ (code: $\NB(k,c/s_i)$): depth multiplies the mean and leaves the shape
        $\kappa$ unchanged, as in scVI. The option \cmfile{'burst_frequency'} uses $\NB(\kappa s_c,r)$.
        Counts stay integers; $s_c$ is stored in \cmfile{obs['depth_factor']} and \texttt{X} is untouched.
  \item \textbf{When it is applied:} the diagnostic recommends the correction if the depth share of the
        mean within-group pairwise correlation exceeds 0.5, or if more than half of the genes have NB modes
        closer than the depth spread. The correlation share is computed on the log1p counts of 1{,}000
        reference genes (the most variable among genes detected in more than 5\,\% of cells), with and
        without the log library size regressed out. The depth spread is the median over groups of
        $q_{0.95}/q_{0.05}$ of $s_c$. The correction is written only if the user allows it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{DeCovarT.} $\SW$ estimated from raw \glsxtrshort{umi} counts mixes both layers. If
$x_{gc}\mid\bm\lambda_{\cdot c},s_c\sim\Pois(s_c\lambda_{gc})$ with $s_c$ independent of $\bm\lambda_{\cdot c}$
within type $j$, the law of total covariance gives (\cref{app:depth})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth}
\Cov(\bm x_{\cdot c})=\E(s^2)\,\bSigma^{\lambda}_j+\Var(s)\,\bmu^{\lambda}_j\bmu^{\lambda\mathsf T}_j
+\E(s)\diag(\bmu^{\lambda}_j)\quad\chk{V9a}.
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Depth} adds a rank-one term along the mean $\bmu^{\lambda}_j$, common to all gene pairs.
        Its biological part (cell size, the $S_j$ of DeCovarT's estimand) aggregates into the bulk and is
        wanted. Its technical part (capture) does not reach the bulk. Because the term lies along the
        type means, an inflated technical share down-weights the very directions that separate the types
        (our reading, not tested). Splitting the two parts needs controls such as spike-ins or ambient
        transcripts \parencite[pp.~8--11]{fangExtrinsicBiologicalStochasticity2025}; CardamomOT's
        diagnostic measures the total depth share only.
  \item \textbf{Poisson sampling} adds to the diagonal only, so it attenuates every correlation and makes
        lowly expressed panels look independent.
  \item \textbf{Bulk scales} $c_i=a_in_i$ and $\phi_i=a_i^2n_i$ absorb a platform scale per bulk sample;
        they do not remove the cell-level depth variance from $\SW$.
  \item \textbf{Raw or denoised in the bulk.} Let $\bSigma^{\mathrm{bio}}_j$ be the covariance of the latent
        expression on the reference scale, so that the raw covariance is
        $\bSigma^{\mathrm{bio}}_j+\diag(\bmu_j)$. If the bulk counts the pooled transcripts of its cells once, at
        depth $a_i$ per reference unit, then
        $\Cov\bm y_{\cdot i}=\phi_i\sum_jp_{ji}\bSigma^{\mathrm{bio}}_j+c_i\diag(\sum_jp_{ji}\bmu_j)$; binomial
        thinning of the reference counts gives the same law \chk{V13a}. The reference Poisson diagonal is
        replaced by the bulk's own, of weight $a_i^{-1}$ per unit of $\phi_i$. The raw $\SW$ is therefore exact
        only at $a_i=1$, and the Poisson-denoised one only as $a_i\to\infty$. At any depth the plug-in is the
        denoised covariance plus $c_i\diag(\sum_jp_{ji}\bmu_j)$, which adds no parameter for UMI-like bulk
        counts \chk{V13b,V13c}. The article reaches the same conclusion in its appendix on the within-type
        covariance that enters the bulk.
  \item \textbf{A model that separates the layers:} the \gls{pln} model, $x_{gc}\sim\Pois(s_c
        e^{z_{gc}})$ with $\bm z_{\cdot c}\sim\mathcal N(\bmu_z,\bSigma_z)$
        \parencite{chiquetPoissonLognormalModelVersatile2021}. Its offset is the measurement layer and
        $\bSigma_z$ the expression layer. Mapped back to the linear scale,
        $\E\bm x=s\,e^{\bmu_z+\diag(\bSigma_z)/2}$ and $\Cov\bm x=\diag(\E\bm x)+(\E\bm x\,\E\bm x\T)\circ
        (e^{\bSigma_z}-1)$, with the exponential taken entrywise, for fixed $s$ \chk{V3a,V3b,V3c}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{CardamomOT's $s_c$ against $\nu^{\mathrm{tech}}$.} CardamomOT's default depth estimator is Fang and
Pachter's size factor on Poisson genes. It estimates the total factor $s_c=s^{\mathrm{bio}}_cs^{\mathrm{tech}}_c$,
and the mode of normalised covariances estimates the total $\nu=\Var s$ (with $\E s=1$), where
$1+\nu=(1+\nu^{\mathrm{bio}})(1+\nu^{\mathrm{tech}})$ \chk{V14a}. DeCovarT's denoised covariance (vignette 05-1,
\texttt{eq-denoised-within}) needs $\nu^{\mathrm{tech}}$ alone. Given $\nu^{\mathrm{tech}}$, it is unbiased once
$\bar{\bm x}\bar{\bm x}\T$ is replaced by $\bar{\bm x}\bar{\bm x}\T-\bm V_j/C_j$ \chk{V14b,V14c,V14e}. Fed with the
total $\nu$, it returns $\bSigma^{\lambda}_j$ and drops
$\nu^{\mathrm{bio}}(\bSigma^{\lambda}_j+\bmu^{\lambda}_j\bmu^{\lambda\mathsf T}_j)$, the cell-size share that a bulk sums
\chk{V14d}. CardamomOT's correction, which divides $s_c$ out of the NB mean, is the count analogue of that
total-$\nu$ plug-in. It suits a network of size-free expression; it does not suit a covariance that must reach
a bulk (our reading).

\textbf{Alternatives to the depth correction.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{scVI} draws a library factor $\ell_n\sim\mathrm{LogNormal}$ whose prior is the empirical mean and
        variance of the log library size in each batch, frequencies $\bm\rho_n$ that sum to one over genes, and
        counts $\Pois(\ell_nw_{ng})$ with $w_{ng}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\rho_{gn},\theta_g)$, plus zero inflation
        \parencite[author manuscript, pp.~8--9]{lopezDeepGenerativeModeling2018}. $\ell_n$ \enquote{represents nuisance variation due to differences in capture
        efficiency and sequencing depth} (p.~3). It is the object $s_c$ estimated inside a likelihood rather than
        on Poisson genes, and it also carries cell size because $\bm\rho_n$ sums to one. Genes are independent
        given a 10-dimensional latent (pp.~3, 8), so regulation would be read from that latent, not from a
        precision matrix (our reading).
  \item \textbf{scANVI} adds a cell-type label with a uniform prior, keeping the scVI library factor and count
        model \parencite[eqs~1--8, p.~15]{xuProbabilisticHarmonizationAnnotation2021}.
        For CardamomOT the label could be a type or a basin.
  \item \textbf{ComBat} corrects \enquote{both additive and multiplicative batch effects} by empirical Bayes,
        per gene and batch \parencite{johnsonAdjustingBatchEffects2007}. Its count
        version ComBat-ref fits $\log\mu_{gij}=\alpha_g+\gamma_{gi}+\beta_{g,\mathrm{cond}(j)}+\log N_j$ (gene $g$, batch $i$, sample $j$) and maps each count to a
        reference batch by matching negative binomial distribution functions
        \parencite[p.~2]{zhangHighlyEffectiveBatch2025}.
        It acts on samples, not cells. It could therefore replace CardamomOT's pairwise rescaling between groups,
        not $s_c$. With time points as batches it removes the time effect unless time enters the model as the
        biological condition.
\end{itemize}

\begin{answerbox}[title=In short: noise layers]
\begin{itemize}
  \item In the paper, CardamomOT's \glsxtrshort{nb} is an expression model with no measurement layer.
        The code of version 2.0.0 adds one: a per-cell depth $s_c$ that multiplies the NB mean, applied
        only when depth explains more than half of the within-group correlation or blurs the NB basins.
  \item DeCovarT's $\SW$ contains a rank-one depth term and a Poisson diagonal. The biological share of
        depth should stay, the technical share should not. CardamomOT's $s_c$ is the total factor, so it cannot
        stand in for $\nu^{\mathrm{tech}}$ \chk{V14d}.
  \item The raw $\SW$ suits a bulk counted at the reference depth per cell ($a_i=1$). Otherwise the bulk needs the
        denoised covariance plus its own Poisson term \chk{V13b}.
  \item DeCovarT can run the depth-share diagnostic on Suppinger before estimating $\SW$.
\end{itemize}
\end{answerbox}

\section{The diagonal and self-regulation (axis iv)}\label{sec:diag}

\textbf{Two different objects.} In a \gls{ggm}, $\omega_{gg}=1/\Var(x_g\mid\bm x_{-g})$ is a
\glslink{def-partial}{conditional precision}, and an edge is a non-zero partial correlation
$-\omega_{gh}/\sqrt{\omega_{gg}\omega_{hh}}$. The diagonal is never an edge. In CardamomOT, $\theta_{gg}$
enters the activation rate of gene $g$ through its own protein (Eq.~1, p.~6): a directed self-loop.

\textbf{Self-activation and bistability.} \enquote{The combination of a positive auto-activation with a
negative basal parameter $\beta_i$ is the most parsimonious way to produce bistability for an isolated
gene} (p.~28). This is the autocatalytic case of \parencite{ferrellSelfperpetuatingStatesSignal2002}: bistability needs feedback, \enquote{some type of non-linearity
within the feedback circuit}, and balanced legs (p.~3 of that review). CardamomOT reports diagonal
coefficients \enquote{systematically non-negligible across all three datasets} (p.~18).

\textbf{Linking the two through the stationary covariance.} Around one basin, linearise the dynamics as
an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process $\dd\bm x=\bm A(\bm x-\bm x^\ast)\dd t+\bm B\,\dd\bm W$. The stationary
covariance solves the \glslink{def-lyapunov}{Lyapunov equation} $\bm A\bSigma+\bSigma\bm A\T+\bm Q=\bm 0$,
$\bm Q=\bm B\bm B\T$. Write $a_g=\gamma_g-\varsigma_g>0$ for the net decay of gene $g$: its degradation
rate $\gamma_g$ minus its linearised self-activation $\varsigma_g$. For two genes with $1\to2$ of strength
$b$ (\cref{app:lyap}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lyap2}
\sigma_{11}=\frac{q_1}{2a_1},\qquad \sigma_{12}=\frac{b\,q_1}{2a_1(a_1+a_2)},\qquad
\omega_{12}=-\frac{2a_2(a_1+a_2)\,b}{(a_1+a_2)^2q_2+b^2q_1}\quad\chk{V5a,V5b}.
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Self-activation acts only through $a_g$: a larger $\varsigma_g$ inflates $\sigma_{gg}$ and the
        conditional variance, so it lowers $\omega_{gg}$. It appears nowhere as a \enquote{self-edge}.
  \item $\omega_{12}\neq0$ if and only if $b\neq0$, but its sign and size mix $b$ with the decays and noise
        intensities, and the direction is lost \chk{V5c}.
  \item With three genes, a fork $1\to2$, $1\to3$ gives $\omega_{23}\propto-b_{21}b_{31}\neq0$: two
        co-regulated targets are linked in the precision although neither regulates the other
        \chk{V5e}. A static \gls{sem} with the same fork would give $\omega_{23}=0$ \chk{V5f}.
  \item The linear model has one basin. Bistability requires the non-linearity that the linearisation
        removes, and produces a bimodal law within a cell type, which a Gaussian within-type law cannot
        represent. DeCovarT's counterpart of a basin is a subtype: more components in the convolution.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What the Lyapunov equation is for.} A snapshot shows cells, not trajectories: it estimates the
stationary law of the dynamics, never the dynamics themselves. Around one basin, $\bm A$ is the Jacobian of
the regulation (off-diagonal: how gene $h$ moves gene $g$; diagonal: minus the net decay $a_g$) and $\bm Q$
the intensity of the intrinsic noise (bursting, molecular noise). The Lyapunov equation is the map from
these dynamic parameters to the second moment that a snapshot estimates, $(\bm A,\bm Q)\mapsto\bSigma$. It
is the bridge between the two estimands: CardamomOT's directed $\bm\theta$ lives in $\bm A$, DeCovarT's
plug-in is $\bSigma$. The map is not invertible: $\bSigma$ has $G(G+1)/2$ free entries, $(\bm A,\bm Q)$ up to
$G^2+G$, so many directed networks share one stationary covariance (our remark).

\textbf{Lyapunov covariance against DeCovarT's denoised covariance.} Add Poisson measurement at a fixed
depth $s$, $x_{gc}\mid\bm\lambda_{\cdot c}\sim\Pois(s\lambda_{gc})$, with $\bm\lambda_{\cdot c}$ drawn from the
stationary law of the basin. By \cref{eq:depth} with $\Var s=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lyap-measure}
\Cov(\bm x_{\cdot c})=s^2\bSigma_{\mathrm{Lyap}}(\bm A,\bm Q)+s\diag(\bmu^{\lambda}),
\end{equation}
so DeCovarT's denoised covariance $\bSigma^{\mathrm{bio}}_j$ estimates $s^2\bSigma_{\mathrm{Lyap}}$, the
stationary covariance, not $\bm A$. Both terms of \cref{eq:lyap-measure} have the form \enquote{structured part
plus diagonal}, but the two diagonals act differently. The intrinsic noise $\bm Q$ enters the dynamics and is
propagated by the network: a noise on gene 1 reaches gene 2 through $b$, which creates $\sigma_{12}$ in
\cref{eq:lyap2}. The measurement diagonal is added after the dynamics and propagates nowhere. Reading $\bm A$
as the biological correlation and the measurement variance as $\bm Q$ would therefore conflate the two
layers of \cref{sec:noise} (our remark). One special case links the precision to the network directly: if
$\bm A$ is symmetric and stable and $\bm Q=q\bm I$, then $\bSigma_{\mathrm{Lyap}}=-\tfrac q2\bm A^{-1}$ and
$\bOmega=-\tfrac2q\bm A$ \chk{V24a}. The off-diagonal of the precision is then the regulation up to sign
and scale, and its diagonal is the net decay, $\omega_{gg}=2a_g/q$. A symmetric $\bm A$ means reciprocal
regulation of equal strength, a gradient system, which a gene regulatory network is in general not.

\textbf{Why $\omega_{gg}$ is not a self-loop.} For any positive-definite $\bOmega$,
$\omega_{gg}=1/\Var(x_g\mid\bm x_{-g})=1/\{\sigma_{gg}(1-R^2_g)\}>0$, where $R^2_g$ is the squared multiple
correlation of gene $g$ on all the other genes \chk{V24c}. The diagonal is always positive, so it cannot carry
the sign of a self-loop (activation or repression). A large $\omega_{gg}$ says that gene $g$ varies little
once the other genes are known: either it is well predicted by them ($R^2_g$ close to one) or its own
variance is small (strong decay, little noise). A small $\omega_{gg}$ says that gene $g$ varies on its own.
Self-activation enters only through that variance: it lowers $a_g$, raises $\sigma_{gg}$ and lowers
$\omega_{gg}$ \chk{V5d}. CardamomOT's \enquote{systematically non-negligible} self-loops (p.~18) and a
large diagonal of $\hat\bOmega_j$ are therefore not the same finding.

\textbf{A directed acyclic graph has a triangular factor.} In a Gaussian Bayesian network, a linear
\gls{sem} $\bm x=\bm B\bm x+\bm e$ with $\bm e\sim\mathcal N(\bm 0,\bm D)$, $\bm D$ diagonal, and an acyclic
graph, ordering the genes so that parents come first makes $\bm B$ strictly lower triangular, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dag-chol}
\bOmega=(\bm I-\bm B)\T\bm D^{-1}(\bm I-\bm B),\qquad
\omega_{gg}=\frac1{d_g}+\sum_{h:\,g\to h}\frac{b_{hg}^2}{d_h},
\end{equation}
the modified Cholesky (LDL) factorisation of $\bOmega$, with a unit triangular factor $\bm I-\bm B$
\chk{V24b} \parencite{pourahmadiJointMeancovarianceModels1999}. Acyclicity forces $b_{gg}=0$: a static
directed acyclic graph has no self-loop, and its $\omega_{gg}$ is the noise precision of gene $g$ plus what its
children reveal about it. Every ordering of the genes gives such a factor, so the order, hence the
direction of the edges, is not identified from $\bOmega$ alone (Markov equivalence); external information,
such as CardamomOT's time points, has to supply it. A self-loop needs time: in a dynamic Bayesian network,
$\bm x_{t}=\bm A\bm x_{t-1}+\bm e_{t}$, the entry $A_{gg}$ is a lag-one effect of gene $g$ on itself, and the
graph unrolled over time stays acyclic. CardamomOT's snapshots are not time series of the same cells, so
such a model would need its couplings between time points, as CardamomOT's transport supplies (our remark). \Cref{app:stat-dag} develops this statistical route: dynamic and mixed Gaussian Bayesian networks,
structure learning and interventions.

\textbf{Penalising the diagonal.} When the graphical lasso penalises every entry, its covariance estimate
has $\hat W_{gg}=V_{gg}+\lambda$ \parencite{friedmanSparseInverseCovariance2008}: every variance is biased
upwards by $\lambda$. DeCovarT's likelihood uses those variances as gene weights, so the diagonal is
never penalised (\texttt{penalize.diagonal = FALSE}). For CardamomOT, the diagonal penalty is a prior
choice. The paper says \enquote{we deliberately choose not to apply a specific penalty on diagonal
coefficients} (p.~28), and suggests zeroing entries of $\bm\theta_0$ when auto-regulation is implausible.
Eq.~10 penalises $\bm\theta-\bm\theta_0$ entrywise. In the code (\cmfile{network.py}), the first
$\ell_1$ step penalises the self-loop like any edge inside the prior support, and the adaptive-lasso
refinement leaves \enquote{self-regulation and basals unpenalized} (\cmfile{refine_inference}). Stating
that two-stage rule in the paper would remove an ambiguity.

\begin{answerbox}[title=In short: diagonal]
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\omega_{gg}$ is a conditional precision; $\theta_{gg}$ is a directed self-loop. They are related
        only through the stationary covariance of a linearised model, where self-activation lowers
        $\omega_{gg}$ \chk{V5d}.
  \item A precision edge is not a regulatory edge: a fork creates one without a direct link.
  \item The Lyapunov equation maps the dynamics $(\bm A,\bm Q)$ to the stationary covariance a snapshot
        estimates; DeCovarT's denoised covariance estimates that covariance, not $\bm A$, and the measurement
        diagonal is added after the dynamics, whereas $\bm Q$ is propagated by them.
  \item $\omega_{gg}=1/\{\sigma_{gg}(1-R^2_g)\}$ is always positive: a large diagonal means a gene well
        predicted by the others, not a self-loop \chk{V24c}. A static \glsxtrshort{sem} has a triangular factor
        and no self-loop \chk{V24b}; self-regulation needs a dynamic model.
  \item Never penalise the \glsxtrshort{ggm} diagonal in DeCovarT. In CardamomOT the self-loop penalty is
        a prior choice, which the code makes differently in its two stages.
\end{itemize}
\end{answerbox}

\section{A side axis (iii): convolution, Wiener filter, mean field}\label{sec:side}

\begin{specbox}
These are analogies to explore, not results. One item is a derivation: the Wiener filter is the
\gls{blup} of a mixed model.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Convolution and characteristic functions.} The bulk is a sum of independent cell
        profiles, so its density is a convolution, and its characteristic function is a product:
        $\varphi_{\bm y}(\bm t)=\prod_j\varphi^{\mathrm{pop}}_j(p_j\bm t)$ for population references. For
        single-cell references, with $\varphi_j$ the characteristic function of one type-$j$ cell, two
        models differ. Given the counts $m_j$ of cells per type, $\varphi_{\bm y}(\bm t)=
        \prod_j\varphi_j(a_i\bm t)^{m_j}$, and the covariance $\phi_i\sum_jp_j\SW$, with $p_j=m_j/n_i$, is
        linear in $\bm p$: this is DeCovarT's single-cell model \chk{V6b}. If each cell is instead drawn
        independently from the type mixture, $\varphi_{\bm y}(\bm t)=\varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}(a_i\bm t)^{n_i}$
        with $\varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}=\sum_jp_j\varphi_j$, and the covariance gains a between-type term
        that is quadratic in $\bm p$ \chk{V6d,V6e} (\cref{app:conv}). DeCovarT's Gaussian is the
        central-limit form of that product. For a non-Gaussian cell law, such as a fitted CardamomOT NB
        mixture, the bulk law of one gene could be computed by \gls{fft} on the count lattice
        \parencite{cooleyAlgorithmMachineCalculation1965}. In several dimensions the lattice grows
        exponentially, so this stays a check for a few genes.
  \item \textbf{Wiener filter as BLUP.} Writing the type-$j$ population profile of the sample as
        $\bmu_j+\bm\varepsilon_{j}$, with $\bm\varepsilon_j\sim\mathcal N(\bm 0,\bSigma_j)$, makes
        $\bm y=\bmu\bm p+\sum_jp_j\bm\varepsilon_j+\bm e$ a \gls{lmm} whose random-effect design depends on
        $\bm p$. Its marginal covariance is $\sum_jp_j^2\bSigma_j+\bm D$ \chk{V1a}, and the
        \glslink{def-blup}{BLUP} of $\bm\varepsilon_j$ (Definitions box) is the Wiener filter of the DeCovarT article \chk{V1b}. With
        $\bm D=\bm 0$, maximum likelihood in $\bm p$, with the $\bSigma_j$ plugged in, is DeCovarT's fit
        \chk{V7a}; a nugget $\bm D\neq\bm 0$, which DeCovarT leaves out (no extra measurement noise),
        changes the likelihood and moves $\hat{\bm p}$ \chk{V7b} (\cref{app:blup}). \Gls{reml} does not
        carry over directly: error contrasts remove $\bm p$ from the mean but not from $\bm V$, which
        depends on $\bm p$ \chk{V7c}. No full Bayesian treatment is needed.
  \item \textbf{Mean field.} Hartree's ansatz writes a many-particle wave function as a product of
        one-particle functions, each in the average field of the others \parencite{hartreeWaveMechanicsAtom1928,fockNaherungsmethodeZurLosung1930}.
        CardamomOT's product over genes given the basin is of that kind: genes interact only through the
        basin label and the protein field. DeCovarT's independent cells are another product ansatz. In
        both, the neglected correlation (Fock's exchange term in physics) is the within-basin covariance
        that CardamomOT reports losing (p.~12).
\end{itemize}
\end{specbox}

\section{What each method could borrow}\label{sec:borrow}

\textbf{DeCovarT from CardamomOT.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The depth-share diagnostic}, run on Suppinger per (time, cell type) before $\SW$ is
        estimated. A large share calls for a decision on the technical part of \cref{eq:depth}, not for
        blind normalisation, since the biological part reaches the bulk.
  \item \textbf{Prior networks as penalty weights} (OmniPath, as in the CardamomOT repository), entering
        an edge-weighted graphical lasso.
  \item \textbf{A basin-separability check} within each type: a gene with separated NB modes inside a type
        signals a subtype that a Gaussian within-type law misrepresents.
  \item \textbf{The panel-as-prior stance} (p.~27): report the panel as part of the model, with the
        sensitivity of $\hat{\bm p}$ to it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{CardamomOT from DeCovarT.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A within-basin covariance.} The basin mixture implies a gene covariance that is diagonal
        plus a between-basin term of rank at most $B-1$ for $B$ basins, by the law of total covariance;
        the attenuation of the strongest correlations (p.~12) is the missing within-basin part. A Gaussian
        copula or a \glsxtrshort{pln} layer within each basin would restore it.
  \item \textbf{A held-out likelihood for the penalty}, next to the quantile universal threshold. The
        expected Gaussian negative log-score $\tfrac12\{\log\det\bSigma+\tr(\bSigma^{-1}\bSigma_{\mathrm{true}})\}$
        is minimised at $\bSigma=\bSigma_{\mathrm{true}}$ whatever the law of the cells, provided its
        second moments are finite and its mean is known \chk{V2a,V2b,V2c,V2d}, and
        cross-validation estimates the Kullback--Leibler risk for penalised estimators as well
        \parencite{commengesDynamicalBiostatisticalModels2015}; \parencite{commengesUniversalApproximateCrossvalidation2012} give a fast approximation.
  \item \textbf{An information criterion for the panel}, aimed at the estimand $\bm\theta$ rather than at
        marginal change in time.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty for $\bm\theta$} beyond seed-to-seed variability, for instance a bootstrap
        over cells within time points.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A.1 Two-gene Lyapunov equation.}\label{app:lyap} With $\bm A=\begin{psmallmatrix}-a_1&0\\
b&-a_2\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $\bm Q=\diag(q_1,q_2)$, the three distinct entries of $\bm A\bSigma+\bSigma
\bm A\T+\bm Q=\bm 0$ read $-2a_1\sigma_{11}+q_1=0$, $b\sigma_{11}-(a_1+a_2)\sigma_{12}=0$ and
$2b\sigma_{12}-2a_2\sigma_{22}+q_2=0$. Hence $\sigma_{11}$ and $\sigma_{12}$ as in \cref{eq:lyap2} and
$\sigma_{22}=\{a_1(a_1+a_2)q_2+b^2q_1\}/\{2a_1a_2(a_1+a_2)\}$. Inverting gives
\[
\omega_{11}=\frac{2\{a_1(a_1+a_2)^2q_2+(a_1+a_2)b^2q_1\}}{q_1\{(a_1+a_2)^2q_2+b^2q_1\}},\qquad
\omega_{22}=\frac{2a_2(a_1+a_2)^2}{(a_1+a_2)^2q_2+b^2q_1},
\]
and $\omega_{12}$ of \cref{eq:lyap2}. For the fork $1\to2$ (strength $b$), $1\to3$ (strength $c$),
$\omega_{23}=-4a_2a_3bc\,q_1(a_1+a_2)(a_1+a_3)(a_2+a_3)/\mathcal D$ with $\mathcal D>0$ a polynomial in the
rates and noise intensities \chk{V5e}: $\mathcal D$ is $b^2c^2q_1^2(a_2-a_3)^2$ plus a polynomial with
positive coefficients. The ledger derives all these forms from the Lyapunov equation \chk{V5a,V5b}.

\paragraph{A.2 Gaussian convolution.}\label{app:conv} Population references: with independent type-$j$
population profiles $\bm x^{\mathrm{pop}}_j\sim\mathcal N(\bmu_j,\bSigma_j)$, the bulk
$\bm y=\sum_jp_j\bm x^{\mathrm{pop}}_j$ has $\varphi_{\bm y}(\bm t)=\prod_j\varphi^{\mathrm{pop}}_j(p_j\bm t)
=\exp\{\mathrm i\,\bm t\T\sum_jp_j\bmu_j-\tfrac12\bm t\T(\sum_jp_j^2\bSigma_j)\bm t\}$, hence the
quadratic covariance. Single-cell references (one type-$j$ cell: mean $\bmu_j$, covariance $\SW$,
characteristic function $\varphi_j$) admit two models. (a) Given $m_j$ cells of type $j$, the bulk has characteristic function $\prod_j\varphi_j(a_i\bm t)^{m_j}$, mean
$a_i\sum_jm_j\bmu_j=c_i\sum_jp_j\bmu_j$ and covariance $a_i^2\sum_jm_j\SW=\phi_i\sum_jp_j\SW$, with the
realised fractions $p_j=m_j/n_i$; it is linear in $\bm p$ \chk{V6b}. This conditional model is
DeCovarT's. (b) If each of the $n_i$ cells is drawn independently from the type mixture, one cell has
characteristic function $\varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}=\sum_jp_j\varphi_j$ and the bulk has
$\varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}(a_i\bm t)^{n_i}$, the average of the product in (a) over
$\bm m\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(n_i,\bm p)$. The law of total covariance then adds
$a_i^2n_i\bmu\{\diag(\bm p)-\bm p\bm p\T\}\bmu\T$ to the covariance of (a); at $J=2$ this is
$\phi_ip(1-p)\bm\Delta\bm\Delta\T$, quadratic in $p$ \chk{V6d,V6e}.

\paragraph{A.3 Wiener filter as BLUP.}\label{app:blup} Stack $\bm u=(\bm\varepsilon_1,\dots,\bm\varepsilon_J)$,
with covariance $\bm\Gamma=\operatorname{blockdiag}(\bSigma_j)$, and let $\bm Z=(p_1\bm I_G,\dots,p_J\bm I_G)$.
Then
$\bm y=\bmu\bm p+\bm Z\bm u+\bm e$, $\bm e\sim\mathcal N(\bm 0,\bm D)$, and $\bm V=\Var\bm y=\bm Z\bm\Gamma
\bm Z\T+\bm D=\sum_jp_j^2\bSigma_j+\bm D$. The \glsxtrshort{blup} is
$\hat{\bm u}=\bm\Gamma\bm Z\T\bm V^{-1}(\bm y-\bmu\bm p)$, whose block $j$ is
$p_j\bSigma_j\bm V^{-1}(\bm y-\bmu\bm p)$: the Wiener filter of the DeCovarT article (\S\,Wiener filter),
equal to the posterior mean in the Gaussian model \chk{V1b,V1c}. Integrating $\bm u$ out gives
$\bm y\sim\mathcal N(\bmu\bm p,\bm V)$. At $\bm D=\bm 0$ its log-likelihood in $\bm p$ is DeCovarT's
Gaussian log-likelihood \chk{V7a}; with $\bm D\neq\bm 0$ the maximiser moves, from $0.747$ to $0.749$ in
a one-gene example with unit nugget \chk{V7b}. For $J=2$, the error contrasts $\bm K\T\bm y$ with
$\bm K\T(\bmu_1-\bmu_2)=\bm 0$ have covariance $\bm K\T\bm V(\bm p)\bm K$, which still depends on $\bm p$,
so \glsxtrshort{reml} does not separate the fixed effect from the variance here \chk{V7c}.

\paragraph{A.4 ANOVA ratio and Fisher information, $J=2$.}\label{app:fisher} One gene, single-cell model:
$y\sim\mathcal N(c\{p\mu_1+(1-p)\mu_2\},\,\phi\{p\sigma_1^2+(1-p)\sigma_2^2\})$, with $c=c_i$ and
$\phi=\phi_i$. For $\mathcal N(m(p),v(p))$, $I(p)=m'(p)^2/v+\tfrac12\{v'(p)/v\}^2$, so
$I(p)=c^2\Delta^2/v+\tfrac12\{(\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2)/(p\sigma_1^2+(1-p)\sigma_2^2)\}^2$. If
$\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2=\sigma^2$, the second term vanishes and $I(p)=(c^2/\phi)\Delta^2/\sigma^2=
n_i\Delta^2/\sigma^2$, since $c^2/\phi=n_i$. With $C_1=C_2=C$ reference cells per type, the
\glsxtrshort{anova} ratio is the plug-in estimate of $(C/2)\Delta^2/\sigma^2$, but its mean is
$\{\nu/(\nu-2)\}\{1+(C/2)\Delta^2/\sigma^2\}$ with $\nu=2C-2$, so $I(p)\approx(2n_i/C)(F-1)$ for large $C$
\chk{V4b}. Over independent genes the Fisher information terms add; with correlated genes they do not in general: for
two genes with correlation $\rho$ they add only at $\rho=0$ or $\rho=2d_1d_2/(d_1^2+d_2^2)$, with
$d_g=\Delta_g/\sigma_g$ \chk{V4e}. Under the population model, $v(p)=
p^2\sigma_1^2+(1-p)^2\sigma_2^2$ depends on $p$ even when $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$, and the proportionality fails:
$I(p)$ becomes affine in $\Delta^2/\sigma^2$, with an offset common to all genes \chk{V4f}.

\textit{Why one gene first, and what changes with several.} The \glsxtrshort{anova} screen ranks genes one
at a time, so the comparison above is made gene by gene. DeCovarT's objective is multivariate. For a panel of
$G$ genes with type covariances $\bSigma_{W,1}$ and $\bSigma_{W,2}$, the single-cell model reads
$\bm y\sim\mathcal N\{c\,\bm m(p),\,\phi\bm S(p)\}$ with $\bm S(p)=p\bSigma_{W,1}+(1-p)\bSigma_{W,2}$, and the
Gaussian Fisher information $\dot{\bm m}\T\bm V^{-1}\dot{\bm m}+\tfrac12\tr\{(\bm V^{-1}\dot{\bm V})^2\}$ (dots for derivatives in $p$) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fisher-multi}
I(p)=n_i\,\bm\Delta\T\bm S(p)^{-1}\bm\Delta+\tfrac12\tr\Bigl[\bigl\{\bm S(p)^{-1}(\bSigma_{W,1}-\bSigma_{W,2})\bigr\}^2\Bigr]
\quad\chk{V23a}.
\end{equation}
The first term is the squared Mahalanobis distance between the type means in the metric of the mixed
covariance; with a common $\bSigma_W$ it is V4d. Correlations change it in either direction: two genes with the
same $\Delta_g/\sigma_g$ and a positive within-type correlation carry less than twice the information of one
(V4e). The second term rewards covariances that differ between the types, whatever the means. It does not grow
with $n_i$, so in a bulk of many cells the covariance acts mainly through the metric $\bm S(p)^{-1}$, as a
\glsxtrshort{gls} weight, and only marginally as a separate source of information (our remark).

The same two ingredients define the overlap of the two type laws, the criterion of the
\vignette{S6-feature-screening-scenario.html}{screening scenario}. The Bhattacharyya coefficient
$\int\sqrt{f_1f_2}=e^{-D_B}=1-H^2$, with $H$ the Hellinger distance, has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bhattacharyya}
D_B=\tfrac18\bm\Delta\T\bar\bSigma^{-1}\bm\Delta+\tfrac12\log\frac{\det\bar\bSigma}
{\sqrt{\det\bSigma_{W,1}\det\bSigma_{W,2}}},\qquad\bar\bSigma=\tfrac12(\bSigma_{W,1}+\bSigma_{W,2})
\quad\chk{V23b},
\end{equation}
which reduces to V8a when the covariances are equal. Minimising the overlap therefore maximises a mean
separation in a covariance metric plus a covariance-difference term, the multivariate counterpart of the
one-gene ratio. The two criteria differ in their object: the overlap is a property of the type laws in the
reference, \cref{eq:fisher-multi} of the composition of a bulk of $n_i$ cells.

\paragraph{A.5 Depth and sampling in the within-type covariance.}\label{app:depth} Conditionally on
$(\bm\lambda,s)$, counts are independent Poisson with means $s\bm\lambda$. Then $\E\bm x=\E(s)\bmu^\lambda$
and $\Cov\bm x=\E\{\Cov(\bm x\mid\bm\lambda,s)\}+\Cov\{\E(\bm x\mid\bm\lambda,s)\}=\E(s)\diag(\bmu^\lambda)+
\Cov(s\bm\lambda)$, with $\Cov(s\bm\lambda)=\E(s^2)\bSigma^\lambda+\Var(s)\bmu^\lambda\bmu^{\lambda\mathsf T}$
when $s$ and $\bm\lambda$ are independent; this is \cref{eq:depth} \chk{V9a}. Dividing an off-diagonal
term by the product of the two means gives Fang \& Pachter's normalised covariance $\Var(s)/(\E s)^2$
for genes with $\Sigma^\lambda_{gh}=0$, whatever the scale of $s$; the same ratio is the normalised
variance $\{\Var(x_g)-\E x_g\}/(\E x_g)^2$ of a gene with $\Sigma^\lambda_{gg}=0$, one of their Poisson
genes (their eq.~5) \chk{V9b}.

\section{Purely statistical approaches to directed networks}\label{app:stat-dag}

This appendix sketches a statistical counterpart to CardamomOT: a directed, signed network with a
likelihood, but without a mechanistic bursting model. The candidate is a mixture of linear Gaussian
\glspl{dbn}. Nothing here is implemented; the aim is to name the tools and the research tracks, with their
conditions. Book pages refer to the printed pages of \textcite{kollerProbabilisticGraphicalModels2009} (KF below).

\subsection{Gaussian Bayesian networks}\label{app:gbn}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Linear Gaussian conditional laws.} Each gene given its parents is
        $x_g\mid\bm x_{\mathrm{pa}(g)}\sim\mathcal N(\beta_{g0}+\bm\beta_g\T\bm x_{\mathrm{pa}(g)},\sigma_g^2)$ (KF Def.~5.14,
        p.~187). A \gls{dag} of such laws defines a joint Gaussian, and conversely every Gaussian is a linear
        Gaussian network for any ordering of the genes (KF Thms~7.3--7.5, pp.~251--253); the network is
        \enquote{not unique}, so its direction is not read from the joint law. In matrix form this is the
        \glsxtrshort{sem} of \cref{eq:dag-chol}.
  \item \textbf{What the precision encodes.} A zero of $\bm J=\bSigma^{-1}$ is a conditional independence given
        all other genes (KF Thm~7.2, p.~251): the edges of the precision are those of the moral graph, not of the
        \glsxtrshort{dag}. Two independent parents of one gene have $\Sigma_{xy}=0$ but $J_{xy}\neq0$ (KF Ex.~7.5,
        p.~254) \chk{V26e}. A sparse \glsxtrshort{dag} can therefore have a dense precision, and a graphical lasso
        returns the moral graph at best.
  \item \textbf{Fitting.} Gaussian laws form an exponential family, and the maximum-likelihood fit of a network
        is the moment-matching projection $\prod_gP(x_g\mid\bm x_{\mathrm{pa}(g)})$ (KF Thms~8.6--8.7,
        pp.~278--281): one regression per gene on its parents.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dynamic Bayesian networks: self-regulation and feedback through time}\label{app:dbn}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Two-slice template.} A \glsxtrshort{dbn} assumes a Markov transition, stationary in time, and
        describes it by a two-slice network over $(\bm x^{(t)},\bm x^{(t+1)})$, unrolled over any horizon (KF
        Defs~6.1--6.4, pp.~201--204). Edges inside a slice must form a \glsxtrshort{dag}; edges between slices
        include persistence edges $x_g^{(t)}\to x_g^{(t+1)}$ (p.~204). The linear Gaussian case is
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:dbn}
        \bm x^{(t+1)}=\bm A\bm x^{(t)}+\bm e^{(t+1)},\qquad\bm e^{(t+1)}\sim\mathcal N(\bm 0,\bm Q)
        \end{equation}
        (KF eqs~6.3--6.4, pp.~211--212), with $A_{gh}\neq0$ an edge $h^{(t)}\to g^{(t+1)}$.
  \item \textbf{Loops without cycles.} The diagonal $A_{gg}$ is a persistence edge: the lag-one effect of a gene
        on itself, the dynamic counterpart of self-regulation. Two edges of opposite direction between slices,
        $h^{(t)}\to g^{(t+1)}$ and $g^{(t)}\to h^{(t+1)}$, form a feedback loop. The unrolled network stays
        acyclic; the loops live in the influence graph, which KF define as cyclic (\enquote{a persistence arc
        $X\to X'$ induces a self-cycle}, Def.~15.2, p.~658). Time also orients these edges: \enquote{In DBNs, we
        cannot reverse an edge $A\to A'$} (p.~837), so the direction that a static network cannot identify
        (\cref{app:gbn}) is fixed between slices.
  \item \textbf{Stationary covariance.} A stable \eqref{eq:dbn} has a stationary covariance that solves the
        discrete Lyapunov (Stein) equation, the counterpart of the continuous equation of \cref{sec:diag},
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:stein}
        \bSigma=\bm A\bSigma\bm A\T+\bm Q,\qquad(\bm I-\bm A\otimes\bm A)\operatorname{vec}\bSigma=\operatorname{vec}\bm Q
        \quad\chk{V26a}.
        \end{equation}
        A symmetric negative loop between two genes (one activates, the other represses, with equal rates)
        gives $\sigma_{12}=0$ but a non-zero lag-one cross-covariance $(\bm A\bSigma)_{12}$ \chk{V26a}: the loop is
        invisible in one snapshot and visible across time (our remark).
  \item \textbf{Inference over time.} The Kalman filter propagates $\bmu^{(t+1)}=\bm A\bmu^{(t)}$ and
        $\bSigma^{(t+1)}=\bm A\bSigma^{(t)}\bm A\T+\bm Q$ (KF eqs~15.4--15.5, pp.~676--677). Over time, every pair of
        state variables becomes dependent (entanglement, Thm~15.1, p.~658), and with switching regimes the exact
        belief state is a Gaussian mixture whose size grows exponentially in $t$ (pp.~683--685).
  \item \textbf{The snapshot problem.} Sequencing destroys the cell, so no cell is observed at $t$ and at
        $t+1$. If only the moments of consecutive snapshots are used, $\bm A$ and
        $\bm A'=\bm A\bm M\bm U\bm M^{-1}$, with $\bm M=\bSigma_t^{1/2}$ and $\bm U$ an orthogonal matrix that fixes
        $\bm M^{-1}\bmu_t$, give the same $\bmu_{t+1}$ and $\bSigma_{t+1}$ \chk{V26b}: two snapshots do not identify
        the network (our remark). Couplings between time points are needed: optimal transport, as in
        CardamomOT; a pseudotime; RNA velocity or metabolic labelling, which measure a time derivative within one
        cell \parencite{lamannoRNAVelocitySingle2018}; or interventions (\cref{app:causal}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Structure learning}\label{app:structure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Constraint-based} methods (PC type) test conditional independences and return an
        I-equivalence class, drawn as a partially directed graph. They need bounded in-degree, faithfulness and
        a reliable test, and are \enquote{sensitive to failures in individual independence tests} (KF
        pp.~785--790).
  \item \textbf{Score-based} methods maximise a penalised likelihood. The likelihood alone favours the complete
        graph (Prop.~18.1, pp.~792--794); BIC, $\ell(\hat\theta_{\mathcal G})-\tfrac12\log M\dim\mathcal G$, is
        consistent (Thm~18.2, pp.~802--803) and decomposes over genes (Def.~18.2, p.~805). The Bayesian score
        with a BDe prior is score-equivalent (Thm~18.4, p.~807), and BGe, its Gaussian counterpart with a
        normal--Wishart prior, is cited only (p.~840). Finding the best graph is NP-hard for in-degree two or more
        (Thm~18.5, p.~811); search uses greedy edge moves, GES, which is consistent under faithfulness
        (pp.~821--822), or MCMC over structures or orders (pp.~829--832).
  \item \textbf{A known order helps.} With the order of the genes known, the best parents of each gene are
        chosen independently (Prop.~18.4, p.~810), polynomial for bounded in-degree. In a \glsxtrshort{dbn}, time
        supplies that order for the edges between slices; with hundreds of genes, a node-wise sparse regression
        (lasso on $\bm A_{g\cdot}$) is the scalable version of the same idea (research track).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mixtures of (dynamic) Bayesian networks}\label{app:bn-mixture}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model.} Each cell $c$ carries a latent regime $z_c$ (a type, a basin), with its own transition
        (\cref{fig:mix-dbn}):
        \begin{equation}\label{eq:mix-dbn}
        z_c\sim\operatorname{Cat}(\bm\pi),\qquad
        \bm x^{(t+1)}_c\mid\bm x^{(t)}_c,z_c=k\sim\mathcal N\bigl(\bm A_k\bm x^{(t)}_c,\bm Q_k\bigr).
        \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Estimation.} The \gls{em} algorithm increases the likelihood at every step and converges to a
        stationary point only (KF Thms~19.3--19.4, pp.~874--875); with Gaussian classes its E-step is a soft
        classification (pp.~875--877). Structural \glsxtrshort{em} also searches the graph of each class: the
        score gain on the completed data bounds the true gain from below (Thm~19.10), but the result is not proved
        to be a local maximum (Algorithm~19.3, p.~922). With missing data, BIC \enquote{tends to underfit badly}
        (p.~915).
  \item \textbf{Mixtures mimic feedback.} If two classes orient one edge in opposite directions, the network
        induced by the mixture is cyclic, \enquote{nevertheless … it specifies a coherent distribution} (KF Box~5.B,
        multinets, pp.~170--171).
  \item \textbf{Pooling creates edges.} A mixture of classes with independent genes is correlated:
        $\Cov(\bm x)=\sum_k\pi_k\bSigma_k+\sum_k\pi_k(\bmu_k-\bar\bmu)(\bmu_k-\bar\bmu)\T$ \chk{V26c}. A network fitted
        on pooled types, time points or basins shows edges that no class has; this is why the basin label matters
        in CardamomOT.
  \item \textbf{Research tracks.} None of the verified methods is both dynamic and built for counts; the switching linear
        dynamical system is the closest dynamic form (KF pp.~683--685).
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \emph{clustNet} clusters samples by a mixture of \glsxtrshortpl{dag}, each component learnt with the
                MCMC samplers of \emph{BiDAG}; the data must be binary or categorical
                \parencite{bayerNetworkbasedClusteringUnveils2025,kuipersEfficientSamplingStructure2022}.
          \item VaMSL learns a mixture of causal \glsxtrshortpl{dag} by variational inference, extending DiBS, with expert
                knowledge as a prior \parencite{bjorkmanIncorporatingExpertKnowledge2026};
                a covariate-dependent mixture lets covariates set the membership probabilities
                \parencite{marchantCovariateDependentMixture2025}.
          \item Identifiability: what can be learnt from data generated by a mixture of \glsxtrshortpl{dag}
                \parencite{saeedCausalStructureDiscovery2020}, and which edges interventions
                identify in such a mixture \parencite{variciInterventionalCausalDiscovery2024}.
          \item pyAgrum's \texttt{BNMixture} is not a latent-class model: it is a list of discrete networks learnt
                separately, with weights set by the user, or a Bayesian bootstrap of one network
                (\texttt{BootstrapMixture}); its use here is to measure the stability of each arc
                \parencite{pyagrumBNMixture2026}.
        \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interventions and causality}\label{app:causal}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Intervention queries.} A causal model is a \glsxtrshort{dag} whose do-operator cuts the edges into
        the intervened genes (KF Def.~21.1, pp.~1014--1015). Observational data identify at best the
        I-equivalence class (pp.~1041--1043); $X\to Y$ and $Y\to X$ can share one Gaussian law and differ under
        $\mathrm{do}(X=x_0)$ \chk{V26d}.
  \item \textbf{Interventional likelihood.} With the genes $Z$ fixed in a sample, the likelihood keeps the
        conditional laws of the other genes only, $\prod_{g\notin Z}P(x_g\mid\bm x_{\mathrm{pa}(g)})$ (KF p.~1044), and
        \enquote{we can now distinguish between I-equivalent models}. With latent confounders the target becomes a
        partial ancestral graph (pp.~1048--1050).
  \item \textbf{A worked method.} \textcite{rauJointEstimationCausal2013} fit one linear Gaussian causal network to observational and knock-out data
        together: each sample $k$ has its own intervention set $J_k$, whose equations drop out of the likelihood
        (their eq.~2, p.~4); Metropolis--Hastings runs over gene orderings with a Mallows proposal, and the
        parameters of each ordering have a closed form. In simulation (10 genes) the AUROC of total effects rose from
        0.749 with observational data alone to 0.948 with one knock-out per gene (their Table~1, p.~6). The method
        has no sparsity, costs $O(p^6)$, and \enquote{feedback loops … cannot be modeled} (pp.~10--11).
  \item \textbf{Relevance to single cells.} A Perturb-seq design gives each cell its own target set $J_c$, which
        is exactly this likelihood. Three gaps remain: CRISPR interference lowers expression rather than fixing
        it (a soft intervention), counts need a count emission, and cell types need the mixture of
        \cref{app:bn-mixture}. KF note the same limit for signalling networks: \enquote{the assumption of
        acyclicity … does not hold for cellular networks} (Box~21.D, pp.~1046--1047), which the dynamic model of
        \cref{app:dbn} relaxes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Undirected models, briefly}\label{app:undirected}

A \gls{ggm} estimates the moral graph, not the regulatory one (\cref{app:gbn}), but it is the better-understood
route in high dimension: joint graphical lassos with group or cooperative penalties share structure across
conditions \parencite{chiquetInferringMultipleGraphical2011,chiquetSparsitySigncoherentGroups2012}, the
\gls{pln} network adds the count layer \parencite{chiquetPoissonLognormalModelVersatile2021}, and the conditions
for support recovery are those of \cref{sec:ceiling} \parencite{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015}. Mixtures of \glsxtrshortpl{ggm} estimate the
clusters and their networks together: SCAN \parencite{haoSimultaneousClusteringEstimation2018}, multiple networks in a Gaussian
mixture \parencite{gaoEstimationMultipleNetworks2016}, AJGM, with a network shared across
clusters \parencite{yangAJGMJointLearning2025}, and BLGGM for
zero-inflated single-cell data \parencite{wuEstimatingHeterogeneousGene2022}; PLNmodels offers mixtures and networks of the \glsxtrshort{pln} model as separate model
variants. A survey of joint estimation is \textcite{tsaiJointGaussianGraphical2022}.

\subsection{What it would give CardamomOT, and open problems}\label{app:stat-open}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gains:} signed, directed edges with a likelihood, hence held-out scores to set the penalty
        (\cref{sec:borrow}), intervals, likelihood-ratio tests, and a direct use of perturbation data.
  \item \textbf{Open problems:} a count emission (Poisson--log-normal rather than Gaussian), sparsity with
        hundreds of genes, the identifiability of a mixture of networks (label switching, classes that differ by
        their means rather than their graphs), and couplings between snapshots, without which the transition is
        not identified \chk{V26b}.
\end{itemize}

        \abstract{%
        \textbf{Introduction.}
        Bulk transcriptomic analyses have contributed to the characterisation of
        tissues and diseases, but each profile averages the expression of every cell
        in the sample, so a shift in cell-type composition and a change of expression
        within a cell type are confounded.
        Single-cell assays separate those signals, yet their cost and the destruction
        of the sample limit them to a few specimens per condition.
        Computational deconvolution estimates the cell-type composition of a bulk
        profile from reference profiles of purified populations or of annotated single
        cells.
        Current methods perform poorly on closely related cell populations, whose
        average profiles nearly coincide, and most return a point estimate without
        uncertainty.
        We hypothesised that the gene--gene covariance of each reference population,
        which encodes its gene-regulatory network, carries the information needed to
        separate them.

        \textbf{Methods.}
        We developed DeCovarT, a generative model of the bulk profile in which the
        expression of each cell type is a latent multivariate Gaussian vector with its
        own mean and its own sparse precision matrix.
        The bulk is the sum of the cell-type profiles weighted by their relative
        abundances, so its law is a weighted Gaussian convolution with an explicit
        likelihood.
        Reference profiles supply the means; the cell-type covariances are estimated
        from single cells by the graphical lasso or by covariance shrinkage, and weights
        on the \(\ell_{1}\) penalty can carry prior mechanistic knowledge.
        We estimate the proportions by maximum likelihood with a Newton--Raphson
        algorithm, after a reparametrisation of the log-likelihood on the isometric
        log-ratio chart that enforces the simplex constraint exactly.
        For a single-cell reference, a second model writes the bulk as a sum of
        independent cells, with two technical scales profiled out of the likelihood.
        The likelihood also supplies Wald, profile-likelihood and parametric-bootstrap
        intervals, and boundary likelihood-ratio tests for the absence of a cell type.

        \textbf{Results.}
        In synthetic scenarios, we changed the overlap between cell-type profiles by
        changing only their gene--gene correlations.
        Larger overlap lowered the accuracy of standard deconvolution methods,
        including quadratic programming as implemented in \emph{DeconRNASeq}, while
        DeCovarT had lower error in the scenarios we evaluated, most clearly when the
        cell-type profiles overlapped strongly.

        \textbf{Use case.}
        Gastruloids are stem-cell aggregates that recapitulate early embryonic
        development, and their viability and morphology vary widely, even among
        structures grown with the same protocol.
        We apply DeCovarT to gastruloid bulk profiles with a single-cell reference, and
        use the estimated proportions to define a time-dependent potency score: how far
        the pluripotent cells present at a snapshot can still give rise to mature cell
        types.

        \textbf{Code and resources.}
        Theory, graphical models and derivations are in the
        \href{https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-04208010}{HAL preprint};
        the R package and its vignettes are on
        \href{https://github.com/bastienchassagnol/DeCovarT}{GitHub}.
        }

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Bulk versus single-cell transcriptomics}
\label{sec:context}

\begin{itemize}
  \item bulk RNA-seq returns one profile per sample, averaging
  measurements over several distinct cell populations
  \item that sum hides cell-specific signal, with two consequences
  \begin{itemize}
    \item loss of specificity: a gene is called differentially expressed when only the
          fraction of the cell type producing it has increased
    \item loss of sensitivity: genes of a minor type are masked by the variable expression
          of a major type
  \end{itemize}
  \item gastruloids: morphotypes diverge along a trajectory of lineage fractions
        and cell states, even among structures grown with the same protocol
        \cite{suppingerMultimodalCharacterizationMurine2023}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item viability and morphotype vary from batch to batch under matched conditions,
          which limits the wider use of organoids
          \todo[inline]{List the biological sources of batch-to-batch variability in gastruloid
          protocols (initial cell number, Wnt-agonist pulse timing, culture medium lot), each with
          a citation from \cite{suppingerMultimodalCharacterizationMurine2023} or its references.}
    \item early cell-type composition is a candidate predictor of the morphotype a
          gastruloid will reach
  \end{itemize}
  \item single-cell RNA-seq resolves types, states and within-type spread at one snapshot
  \begin{itemize}
    \item it names the driver of a failed structure: a missing lineage, a stalled state,
          or extra variance inside a type
    \item cost and analysis burden limit cohorts to a few structures per condition
    \item dissociation destroys the gastruloid and each structure yields few cells, so one
          gastruloid cannot be followed through time; every profile is a unique snapshot
  \end{itemize}
  \item developmental cells lie on a continuum of differentiation with graded potency,
        unlike mature blood cells that fall into discrete lineages; neighbouring states
        then share most of their mean expression
  \item deconvolution bridges the two technologies: a single-cell reference supplies
        type-specific profiles, and cheap bulk samples supply the cohort
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Related deconvolution methods}
\label{sec:gap}



Every method below writes the bulk on a linear scale,
\(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}=\sum_{j}p_{ji}\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j,i}\).
The methods differ in the law of the purified profiles, in whether genes are
independent, and in whether the variance of type \(j\) enters the bulk with
weight \(p_{ji}\) (independent cells) or \(p_{ji}^{2}\) (one random population
profile).
Methods that infer cell-type-specific expression given the composition, and
semi-reference and reference-free methods, are reviewed in Appendix~\ref{app:review}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item regression on reference means is the baseline:
        \emph{CIBERSORT} \cite{newmanRobustEnumerationCell2015,newmanDeterminingCellType2019},
        \emph{DeconRNASeq} \cite{gongDeconRNASeqStatisticalFramework2013},
        \emph{MuSiC} \cite{wangBulkTissueCell2019},
        \emph{DWLS} \cite{tsoucasAccurateEstimationCelltype2019},
        \emph{Bisque} \cite{jewAccurateEstimationCell2020},
        \emph{deMixSC} \cite{guoDeMixSCDeconvolutionFramework2023}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item shared assumption: \(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}]=\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\)
          with independent genes; profile variability enters, at most, as a fixed weight per gene
    \item \emph{MuSiC} weights gene \(g\) by the inverse of a between-subject variance;
          \emph{DWLS} by the inverse squared fitted mean, capped by a dampening constant, so that rare and
          abundant types are estimated with comparable relative error;
          \emph{Bisque} learns a gene-wise affine map from bulk to pseudo-bulk
    \item \emph{deMixSC} keeps weighted NNLS and combines the \emph{DWLS} and \emph{MuSiC} weights; a matched bulk and
          single-nucleus benchmark selects platform-sensitive genes, which are down-weighted rather than corrected
    \item all these weighted least-squares fits are generalised-method-of-moments estimators: they solve sample
          moment conditions and return a point estimate, with no interval unless a variance model, a sandwich or a
          resampling unit is added
    \item no measurement model: least squares on intensities, often after TPM or CPM normalisation
    \item that normalisation is a \gls{closure}: the profile is divided by its own total, so the
          expression of the last gene is fixed by the others, the covariance becomes singular, and
          genes that were independent become negatively correlated
          \cite{aitchisonStatisticalAnalysisCompositional1982}; for independent genes with
          variances \(v_{g}\), normalised mean \(z_{g}\) and library size \(L\), the delta method gives
          \(\operatorname{Cov}(z_{g},z_{g'})\approx-(z_{g}v_{g'}+z_{g'}v_{g}-z_{g}z_{g'}\sum_{h}v_{h})/L^{2}\) for \(g\neq g'\)
          (Appendix~\ref{app:alignment}); the gene network estimated on raw counts is then no longer the
          network of the normalised bulk, and the target becomes transcript fractions
    \item output: a point estimate on the simplex, from \(\nu\)-support vector regression,
          quadratic programming or weighted non-negative least squares (NNLS)
          \cite{dessoleLawsonHansonAlgorithmDeviation2023}
  \end{itemize}
  \item univariate distribution-aware likelihoods:
        \emph{RNA-Sieve} \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021},
        \emph{ReDeconv} \cite{luTranscriptomeSizeMatters2025},
        \emph{RCTD} \cite{cableRobustDecompositionCell2022a},
        \emph{DeconV} \cite{gynterDeconvProbabilisticCellType2023}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item the bulk is a sum of independent cells, so a type's variance enters linearly in
          \(p_{ji}\); genes stay independent
    \item the number of cells per observation decides the law: a spatial spot holds a handful of
          cells and a few hundred molecules, so the count distribution and its mean--variance
          relationship must be modelled explicitly for unbiased and consistent estimates; a bulk
          sample sums thousands to millions of cells, which puts each gene in the central-limit
          regime, and when the target is the mean composition with averaged cell-type moments
          plugged in, a Gaussian with the first two moments suffices; \emph{RNA-Sieve},
          \emph{DECALS} and \emph{ReDeconv} rely on this device
          \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021,caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024,luTranscriptomeSizeMatters2025}
    \item \emph{RNA-Sieve} and \emph{ReDeconv} use a central-limit Gaussian per gene;
          \emph{RCTD}, designed for spatial spots, a Poisson law with log-normal overdispersion and a
          gene-wise platform ratio \(\tilde b_{g}=e^{\gamma_{g}}\) estimated in a first stage
          \cite{cableRobustDecompositionCell2022a}; \emph{DeconV} a Poisson bulk
    \item \emph{RNA-Sieve} models noisy reference means across several bulks and returns
          Fisher or Godambe confidence regions; \emph{RCTD} and \emph{ReDeconv} return
          point estimates
  \end{itemize}
  \item multivariate, covariance-aware methods:
        \emph{DECALS} \cite{caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024} and
        \emph{MEAD} \cite{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{DECALS} assumes \(\operatorname{Cov}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i})=\sum_{j}p_{ji}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\),
          the first two moments of DeCovarT without a density;
          it estimates \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) by regressing residual outer products
          on \(\hat p_{ji}^{2}\) across bulks, then returns constrained least squares with a
          sandwich-form covariance whose meat is the model-based \(\sum_{j}\hat p_{ji}^{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\)
    \item \emph{MEAD} is an errors-in-variables model with a sample scale and a random
          gene-wise platform ratio; it returns delta-method intervals as \(G\to\infty\) under
          an almost-sparse dependence graph estimated from the bulk
    \item both need many bulk samples of varying composition; in \emph{MEAD} the gene
          dependence enters only the sandwich, not the point estimate
  \end{itemize}
  \item Bayesian estimation of proportions:
        \emph{DSection} \cite{erkkilaProbabilisticAnalysisGene2010},
        \emph{BLADE} \cite{andrade-barbosaBayesianLogNormalDeconvolution2021},
        \emph{BEDwARS} \cite{ghaffariBEDwARSRobustBayesian2023},
        \emph{BayesPrism} \cite{chuCellTypeGeneExpression2022}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Dirichlet prior on \(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\), linear mixing, profiles drawn around
          the reference (Gaussian, log-normal or a single-cell prior)
    \item posterior summaries by Gibbs, Metropolis--Hastings or variational inference;
          none models a \(G\times G\) gene covariance
  \end{itemize}

  \end{itemize}
  
Most of these methods treat the reference signature as a fixed design.
The bulk is regressed on \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) as in ordinary least squares,
\(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}=\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}\),
with an error assumed independent of the regressors and with a law that does not depend on
\(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\): the \gls{exogeneity} assumption of linear regression.
In contrast to standard linear regression, DeCovarT relaxes exogeneity by treating latent
cell-type expression profiles as random rather than fixed.
The residual \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}=\sum_{j}p_{ji}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j,i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j})\)
still has mean zero, but its covariance \(\sum_{j}p_{ji}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) depends on the unknown
composition, so the noise itself carries information on \(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\).
DeCovarT shares this view with \emph{RNA-Sieve}, \emph{DECALS}, \emph{MEAD} and the Bayesian methods,
but most fixed-signature engines use neither the within-reference (cell-to-cell) nor the
between-reference (donor-to-donor) variability of the cell-type profiles.

Three gaps remain.
First, no reference-based method uses a type-specific gene--gene covariance to shape the
point estimate itself, which is where closely related cell types differ.
Second, few methods return calibrated intervals for a single bulk sample, and none handles a
proportion on the boundary of the \gls{simplex}, where one or several cell types are absent
(active null proportions).
Third, estimating \(\boldsymbol{p}\) jointly with \(\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\}\) and the technical noise from the
same bulk samples is weakly identified; unlike \emph{MEAD}, \emph{DECALS} and \emph{RNA-Sieve}, which
estimate some of these terms from the bulk cohort, DeCovarT plugs in
\((\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\})\) from the reference and estimates \(\boldsymbol{p}\) only.

\subsection{DeCovarT}
\label{sec:decovart}

\begin{itemize}
  \item model: each cell-type profile is a multivariate Gaussian with a sparse precision
        (a gene-regulatory network), and the bulk is their weighted convolution, itself a
        multivariate Gaussian with an explicit likelihood
        \cite{chassagnolDeCovarTMultidimensionalProbalistic2023}
  \item reference second moments: five estimators of the cell-type-specific covariance are
        compared on a single-cell reference (Section~\ref{sec:cov-estimation}): partial
        correlations, the graphical lasso with an unpenalised diagonal, a de-sparsified
        graphical lasso with false discovery rate control, a Poisson--log-normal network,
        and a dense shrinkage covariance
  \item estimation: constrained maximum likelihood on the isometric log-ratio (ILR) chart
        of the simplex \cite{egozcueIsometricLogratioTransformations2003}
  \item inference: Wald and profile-likelihood intervals for each proportion, and boundary
        likelihood-ratio tests for the absence of one or several cell types
        (active null proportions) with chi-bar-square null laws
        \cite{selfAsymptoticPropertiesMaximum1987}
  \item a synthetic simulator that controls the difficulty of each scenario
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Gram construction of the mean signature to a target cosine
    \item random network topologies for the precision of each type
    \item a covariance scale tuned to a target MixSim overlap
          \cite{melnykovMixSimPackageSimulating2012,rianiSimulatingMixturesMultivariate2015}:
          the probability of misassigning a cell from its own profile, hence a lower bound
          on the error of any composition estimate built from those profiles
    \item normalised Shannon entropy of \(\boldsymbol{p}\) to control imbalance
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{itemize}
    \item Section~\ref{sec:results}: three synthetic settings (two genes, twenty genes with network-driven
          covariances, and a single-cell reference with two lineages), then the gastruloid application
    \item Section~\ref{sec:methods}: the two generative models, their optimisation and inference, the simulator,
          and how a single-cell reference is turned into DeCovarT inputs

\subsection{Bivariate toy: two genes, two cell types}
\label{sec:bivariate}

\begin{itemize}
  \item purpose: check that gene--gene correlation changes the estimated fractions
        when only two genes and two cell types are observed
  \item one free ILR coordinate on the simplex;
        the likelihood is a curve, then mapped back to \(\boldsymbol{p}\)
  \item each purified profile is Gaussian;
        the bulk is their abundance-weighted sum, with no extra noise
        \eqref{eq:convolution}
  \item \(G=2\), \(J=2\), \(972\) scenarios; \(N=200\) Monte Carlo bulks per scenario and
        \(B=100\) bootstrap refits per bulk by default, both set by environment variables
        (the figures of the repository come from a dry run with \(N=B=5\))
  \item composition \(\boldsymbol{p}\): balanced \((1/2,1/2)\),
        moderately unbalanced \((17/20,3/20)\),
        highly unbalanced \((99/100,1/100)\)
  \item centroid gap: small, \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot 1}=(20,22)\) versus
        \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot 2}=(22,20)\);
        large, \((20,40)\) versus \((40,20)\)
  \item within-type correlation \(\rho_{j}\in\{-0.8,-0.6,\ldots,0.8\}\),
        set separately for each type
  \item marginal variances: homoscedastic ($\sigma^{2}=1$ for both genes) or heteroscedastic
        ($\sigma_{1}^{2}=1$, $\sigma_{2}^{2}=2$)
  \item methods
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{DeconRNASeq} (\texttt{lsei}) \cite{gongDeconRNASeqStatisticalFramework2013}: least squares on the
          closed simplex
    \item non-negative least squares (NNLS) \cite{dessoleLawsonHansonAlgorithmDeviation2023}, renormalised afterwards
    \item DeCovarT with two optimisers of the same likelihood: Newton--Raphson in ILR coordinates with a trust
          region, and L-BFGS-B on the closure of the non-negative orthant, which can return exact zeros
          (Section~\ref{sec:optimisation})
    \item DeCovarT with the Jeffreys (Firth) penalty \cite{firthBiasReductionMaximum1993}
    \item Marquardt--Levenberg, the ILR gradient method and simulated annealing stay in the package but leave
          the benchmark: they maximise the same likelihood as Newton--Raphson, more slowly
  \end{itemize}
  \item NNLS and \texttt{lsei} differ: \texttt{lsei} keeps the sum-to-one equality and the bounds at every iteration,
        whereas NNLS followed by a renormalisation is least squares on the simplex with a free bulk scale, \(\boldsymbol{\beta}=c\,\boldsymbol{p}\) with \(c=\mathbf{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}\), whereas
        \texttt{lsei} fixes \(c=1\); the two coincide only when \(\hat c=1\); over 500 bulks they differed by a median of
        \(0.002\) (95th percentile \(0.015\)) in the largest coordinate here, where \(\hat c\) stays within about \(0.03\)
        of one, but by a median of \(0.03\) (95th percentile \(0.11\), maximum \(0.19\)) for twenty genes, three types
        and \(\boldsymbol{p}=(0.84,0.08,0.08)\), where \(\hat c\) varies by \(0.13\); the true scale of a population bulk is
        one, so \texttt{lsei} uses information that the free-scale fit discards, whereas the scale of a single-cell bulk
        is unknown and the fixed scale of \texttt{lsei} is then misspecified (Section~\ref{sec:sc-hierarchical}); a robust
        linear model is not run on two genes
  \item the second-generation methods (\emph{MEAD}, \emph{DECALS}, \emph{RNA-Sieve}, \emph{ReDeconv}) are not run
        on two genes: their variance estimates and their asymptotics need many more genes than cell types; nor is
        \emph{CIBERSORT}, whose \(\nu\)-SVR would regress two bulk values on two signatures and an intercept
  \item confidence intervals at every DeCovarT estimate
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Wald on \(\boldsymbol{p}\) by the delta method, which can leave \([0,1]\)
    \item Wald in ILR coordinates mapped back to the simplex
    \item profile likelihood, invariant to the chart and inside the simplex
    \item parametric bootstrap percentile interval, refitted with the optimiser that produced the estimate
    \item for Newton--Raphson, the oracle half-width computed from the information at the true composition,
          the width an analyst would report with the true information
    \item Firth: Wald and ILR intervals at the penalised estimate
    \item reported per interval: coverage of the true fraction with a Wilson Monte Carlo interval, mean and median
          width, and the share of Wald intervals that leave \([0,1]\)
  \end{itemize}
  \item code and outputs: \repo{blob/main/scripts/fig02_bivariate_toy.R}{script},
        \vignette{fig02-bivariate-toy.html}{vignette},
        \repo{tree/main/output/fig02}{plot books};
        \texttt{fig02\_benchmark.rds} holds the settings, the design, the truth and every metric table, and
        \texttt{fig02\_plot\_data.rds} the data behind each figure, keyed by its file name;
        file-by-file reading in Appendix~\ref{app:fig02}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Covariance-driven scenario: twenty genes, three cell types}
\label{sec:hd}

The bivariate toy isolates the effect of gene--gene covariance in a
fully transparent setting but cannot demonstrate scalability or the
realistic topology of gene-regulatory networks (GRNs). We therefore
designed a second scenario with $G=20$ genes and $J=3$ cell types
(Figure~\ref{fig:hd}).
Cell types~1 and~2 are near mean-collinear (cosine \(0.9\)), so mean-only methods
hardly separate them; the network topology encoded in
$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}$ carries the remaining discriminative signal.

\begin{itemize}
  \item role: two types with nearly collinear means, so the discriminative signal sits in
        the precision graphs (design in Section~\ref{sec:synthetic})
  \item \(G=20\), \(J=3\), seed \texttt{20260807}
  \item one Gram: cosines \(0.9\) (types 1--2) and \(0.1\) (either of those versus type 3);
        scale \(s_{\mu}=10\), so the Euclidean gaps are about \(4.5\) and \(13.4\)
  \item types 1 and 2: scale-free or stochastic block model graphs, \(2\times 2\) assignments
        \cite{barabasiEmergenceScalingRandom1999,hollandStochasticBlockmodelsFirst1983};
        type 3: scale-free, held fixed
  \item strict positive definiteness by a spectral shift of the precision matrices
        (\ref{eq-affine-spectrum-transformation}) \cite{chiquetVariationalInferenceSparse2018}
  \item one global covariance scale per graph assignment, chosen so that the average overlap is \(0.02\), \(0.08\) or
        \(0.20\) at the balanced composition; an unbalanced \(\boldsymbol{p}\) raises the realised overlap, while the zeros
        of every precision matrix are kept
  \item compositions from normalised Shannon entropy \(H^{\star}\in\{1,0.5,0.1\}\):
        \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\), \((0.84,0.08,0.08)\) and \((0.98,0.01,0.01)\);
        \(4\times3\times3=36\) scenarios
  \item \(N=100\) bulks per scenario, \(B=200\) bootstrap refits and a reference panel of \(R=N\) individuals by
        default (environment variables; dry run \(N=B=R=5\))
  \item methods: \emph{DeconRNASeq} (\texttt{lsei}), NNLS, the robust linear model of \cite{monacoRNASeqSignaturesNormalized2019}
        (\texttt{MASS::rlm}) and \emph{CIBERSORT} \cite{newmanRobustEnumerationCell2015} on the true means; DeCovarT (Newton--Raphson, L-BFGS-B, Firth) on the true moments, with the intervals of
        Section~\ref{sec:bivariate}
  \item second-generation methods, each run from its authors' code with gene selection and preprocessing filters
        switched off and the other defaults kept
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{MEAD} \cite{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026} (R package, commit \texttt{d66fa10}): estimates the reference
          means and their between-individual covariance from per-individual profiles, with its sandwich interval;
          it rescales every reference profile to a unit total, so its proportions are transcript fractions, and its
          intervals are scored against both the cell and the transcript fractions
    \item \emph{RNA-Sieve} \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021} (Python \texttt{rnasieve} 0.1.4): joint
          fit over the bulks of a scenario; its released interval code returns \([0,1]\) whenever the averaged Hessian has a
          condition number above \(10^{6}\), because it then computes \(\boldsymbol{H}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{H}^{\top}\) instead of
          \(\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{H}^{-\top}\) on a subsample of genes (in the dry run of this scenario that branch never fired,
          and the released and recomputed Godambe intervals coincide); we therefore also score the two
          intervals of the paper, recomputed on all genes with the package's own per-gene scores and averaged Hessian: the
          Wald interval from the inverse empirical Fisher information \(\boldsymbol{B}^{-1}/G\) and the Godambe interval
          \(\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{H}^{-\top}/G\), with \(\alpha_{K}=1-\sum_{k<K}\alpha_{k}\) and the variance of \(\alpha_{K}\)
          equal to \(\mathbf{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{V}\mathbf{1}\) (Appendix~\ref{app:mle-regularity})
    \item \emph{ReDeconv} \cite{luTranscriptomeSizeMatters2025} (Python \texttt{redeconv} 1.2.1): six reweighted least-squares
          steps on the true means and standard deviations; no interval
    \item \emph{DECALS} \cite{caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024} has no public implementation and is not run; its point
          estimate, constrained least squares solved as a quadratic programme, is the \texttt{lsei} baseline
  \end{itemize}
  \item protocol of the competitors: \emph{MEAD} and \emph{RNA-Sieve} read a reference panel of \(R\) purified profiles per
        type drawn independently of the bulks (reusing the profiles that built a bulk would leak its composition) and
        are fitted on the cohort of the \(N\) bulks of a scenario, whereas DeCovarT fits one bulk at a time
  \item code and outputs: \repo{blob/main/scripts/fig03_variance_covariance_driven.R}{script},
        \vignette{fig03-covariance-driven.html}{vignette}, \repo{tree/main/output/fig03}{plot books}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Biological application: gastruloid development}
\label{sec:gastruloid}

\begin{itemize}
  \item dataset: murine gastruloids with a multimodal characterisation at 48, 72 and 96 hours
        \cite{suppingerMultimodalCharacterizationMurine2023}; single-cell reference and bulk
        profiles described in Section~\ref{sec:suppinger}
  \item why gastruloids test deconvolution
  \begin{itemize}
    \item high batch-to-batch variability of viability and morphotype under matched protocols
    \item developmental states form a potency gradient, so adjacent states share most of their
          mean expression; mean-only engines are expected to confuse them
    \item one dominant population at early stages, with several rare types
  \end{itemize}
  \item comparison set: DeCovarT against the mean-only and distribution-aware engines of
        Section~\ref{sec:gap}, on the same genes and the same reference
  \item ablation design, run as a factorial on pseudo-bulks of known composition built from
        held-out cells
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item gene-selection stringency: the presence filter plus the cell-type main-effect test of
          Section~\ref{sec:feature-selection}, at increasing effect-size thresholds that yield
          nested panels from a few dozen to several thousand genes
    \item covariance or precision estimator: the five strategies of
          Section~\ref{sec:cov-estimation} (partial correlations, graphical lasso with an
          unpenalised diagonal, de-sparsified graphical lasso, Poisson--log-normal network,
          dense shrinkage), plus the diagonal as a mean-only control
    \item single-cell to bulk alignment: the model of Section~\ref{sec:sc-tailored} against the
          population-level quadratic-weight model and the alternatives of Appendix~\ref{app:alignment}
    \item engines: DeCovarT and the comparison set, each at every panel size
  \end{enumerate}
  \item expected pattern, to be tested
  \begin{itemize}
    \item too few genes: every engine fails, because the mean signature is rank deficient for
          close types and the covariance has too few coordinates
    \item many genes, with noisy and platform-dependent genes removed: engines converge to
          similar accuracy, because the mean signature alone becomes injective
    \item an intermediate range, or scenarios with strongly correlated types, where
          covariance-driven engines outperform mean-only ones
    \item the result is a calibration curve of accuracy against panel size, stratified by
          the overlap of the purified types
  \end{itemize}

\subsection{Population-level references: DeCovarT as a Gaussian convolution}
\label{sec:convolution-model}

\begin{itemize}
  \item setting: each reference profile is a pooled population, a sorted bulk library or a microarray, as for
        \emph{DSection}, \emph{DeMix} and \emph{DeMixT}
        \cite{erkkilaProbabilisticAnalysisGene2010,ahnDeMixDeconvolutionMixed2013,wangTranscriptomeDeconvolutionHeterogeneous2018}
  \item a pooled profile averages its cells, so only its fluctuation across specimens is observed: within-type
        cell-to-cell variability and between-specimen variability are confounded in \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\)
        (\vignette{theory-single-cell-generative-optimisation.html\#sec-facs}{single-cell generative and optimisation vignette})
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Generative model and core assumptions}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Set-up.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item bulk expression \(\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{gi})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{G\times N}\), with profile
        \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{G}\) for sample \(i\); samples are independent
  \item mean signature \(\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{gj})\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times J}\); as in \emph{MuSiC}
        \cite{wangBulkTissueCell2019}, \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}\) stores the mean profile of cell type \(j\)
        across reference samples or cells, not a single draw
  \item unknown proportions \(\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{ji})\), with composition \(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\) of sample \(i\)
  \item covariance \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times G}\) and \gls{precision}
        \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}\equiv\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}^{-1}\) of cell type \(j\): one network per type, stacked into a
        \(G\times G\times J\) tensor
  \item most deconvolution models write the bulk as the linear combination of the mean profiles,
        with each composition on the \gls{simplex}, the setting of constrained least-squares simplicial
        regression \cite{tsagrisConstrainedLeastSquares2025}:
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}=\boldsymbol{\mu}\,\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},
  \qquad
  \sum_{j=1}^{J}p_{ji}=1,
  \qquad
  p_{ji}\ge 0
  \quad(i=1,\ldots,N)
  \label{eq-linear-deconvolution}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Core modelling assumptions.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(A\arabic*)]
  \item linear mixing on a linear scale: \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}=\sum_{j}p_{ji}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j,i}\), with
        counts or intensities, never logarithms
  \item random cell-type profiles: \(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j,i}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})\), which
        relaxes exogeneity (Section~\ref{sec:gap})
  \item independence of the profiles across cell types, so the bulk law is a Gaussian \gls{convolution}
  \item plug-in reference moments \((\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\})\), each \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) \gls{posdef}
  \item no platform scale and no extra measurement noise: bulk and reference profiles are on one scale
  \item one fit per bulk sample
\end{enumerate}
Under (A1)--(A4), the implied law is the estimating equation of DeCovarT; derivatives, the ILR
chain rule and the Fisher information are in Appendix~\ref{app:derivation} and the
\vignette{theory-population-generative-optimisation.html}{population generative and optimisation vignette}.

\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}
  \sim
  \mathcal{N}\!\left(
    \boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},\;
    \sum_{j=1}^{J} p_{ji}^{2}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}
  \right)
  \label{eq:convolution}
\end{equation}

with log-likelihood, for one sample and \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{p})=\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\),
\begin{equation}
  \ell(\boldsymbol{p})
  =C-\frac{1}{2}\log\det\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{p})
  -\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{p})^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p})
  \label{eq-loglikelihood-multivariate-gaussian}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item the factor \(p_{ji}^{2}\) is the variance of a scaled random population profile;
        \(\boldsymbol{S}_{B}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})\) in Table~\ref{tab:notation} is this quadratic mixture
  \item plug-in \((\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\})\); the unknown is \(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\) only
  \item the determinant term penalises compositions that inflate the covariance, while the
        Mahalanobis term is a generalised least-squares residual whose weight matrix moves with
        \(\boldsymbol{p}\)
  \item a sparse \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}^{-1}\) encodes a Gaussian Markov graph
        \cite{friedmanSparseInverseCovariance2008,chiquetVariationalInferenceSparse2018}, but the bulk
        precision \((\sum_{j}p_{ji}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})^{-1}\) is dense in general
  \item identifiability: \(\boldsymbol{p}\) is identified when
        \(\boldsymbol{p}\mapsto(\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p},\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})\) is
        injective; \(\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=J\) is sufficient but not necessary, since types
        with collinear means stay identified when their covariances differ
  \item Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} compares these assumptions with the closest likelihood- and
        moment-based methods
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{In silico mixtures}
\label{sec:synthetic}

\begin{itemize}
  \item purified profiles \(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})\)
        are combined by the convolution \eqref{eq:convolution}, not drawn from a finite mixture;
        five layers are generated separately (pipeline in the
        \vignette{theory-synthetic-scenarios-mean-covariance.html}{synthetic-scenarios vignette})
  \item mean signature with a target Gram matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:scenarios}A)
  \begin{itemize}
    \item target cosines in \(R\), by default equicorrelation \(R_{J}(\rho)=(1-\rho)\mathbf{I}_{J}+\rho\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\)
    \item \(\boldsymbol{\mu}=s_{\mu}\mathbf{Q}R^{1/2}\) with an orthonormal frame \(\mathbf{Q}\) and the symmetric
          spectral root, so \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mu}=s_{\mu}^{2}R\) exactly; a disjoint-support frame
          gives \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\ge 0\) for quadratic-programming baselines
  \end{itemize}
  \item precision graphs
  \begin{itemize}
    \item skeletons: preferential-attachment scale-free \cite{barabasiEmergenceScalingRandom1999},
          stochastic block model \cite{hollandStochasticBlockmodelsFirst1983}, small-world
          \cite{wattsCollectiveDynamicsSmallworld1998}, Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi or hub graphs; scale-free graphs
          stress degree heterogeneity and are not a biological default \cite{broidoScalefreeNetworksAre2019}
    \item signed weights \(\pm v\) with an inhibitory fraction \(\pi_{-}\), then a spectral shift
          \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{W}+(|\lambda_{\min}(\boldsymbol{W})|+u)\mathbf{I}\) that keeps the support and signs
  \end{itemize}
  \item global covariance scale \(s\): replacing \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) by \(s\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) keeps every
        structural zero and partial correlation, and only shrinks the Mahalanobis separation of the means;
        the covariance share of the Fisher information rises with \(s\)
  \item composition: one-dominant family \((1-(J-1)q,q,\ldots,q)\) solved for a target normalised entropy
        \(H^{\star}(\boldsymbol{p})=-\sum_{j}p_{j}\log p_{j}/\log J\)
  \item scenario at a target overlap \(\bar\omega^{\star}\)
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item fix \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\), the graphs, signs, weights and cushion, giving base covariances
          \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(0)}_{j}\); calibrate at the barycentre
    \item solve \(\bar\omega(s)=\bar\omega^{\star}\) on \(s\in[10^{-3},10^{3}]\) by Brent's method, with the
          same Monte Carlo points at every evaluation (common random numbers) so the objective is
          deterministic in \(s\)
    \item return \(s^{\star}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(0)}_{j}\); held fixed are the means, supports, signs and partial
          correlations
  \end{enumerate}
  \item overlap estimator
  \begin{itemize}
    \item pairwise MixSim overlap
          \(\omega_{j\ell}=\Pr_{f_{j}}(\pi_{\ell}f_{\ell}>\pi_{j}f_{j})+\Pr_{f_{\ell}}(\pi_{j}f_{j}>\pi_{\ell}f_{\ell})\),
          averaged over pairs into \(\bar\omega\) \cite{maitraSimulatingDataStudy2010,melnykovMixSimPackageSimulating2012}
    \item exact route for \(G<4\) (\texttt{MixSim::overlap()}); for \(G\ge4\), our Monte Carlo route draws
          \(n\) points per component from a scrambled Sobol sequence mapped through \(\Phi^{-1}\) and the
          Cholesky factor of \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\), and counts Bayes-rule misassignments
          (\vignette{theory-distance-covariance.html}{distance vignette})
    \item Monte Carlo error is close to the binomial \(\sqrt{\omega(1-\omega)/n}\): standard deviations of
          \(0.0008\) to \(0.0032\) with \(n=4000\) over the three fig03 targets, and agreement with the
          exact route to within one standard deviation on a three-gene block
          \todo{Confirm these Monte Carlo errors with the script seed and report them in the vignette.}
    \item for two equally weighted components, \(\omega=1-\operatorname{TV}(f_{j},f_{\ell})\); the closed-form
          Hellinger distance brackets it, \(1-H\sqrt{2-H^{2}}\le\omega\le1-H^{2}\), and is kept as a cheap descriptor
  \end{itemize}
  \item descriptors reported with every scenario, with no composite score (formulas, bounds and what each
        captures: Table~\ref{tab:descriptors})
  \begin{itemize}
    \item composition: \(H^{\star}\), effective number of types, \(\lVert\boldsymbol{p}\rVert_{2}^{2}\)
    \item mean geometry: pairwise cosines, Euclidean gaps, condition number of \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\)
    \item conditioning of \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{p})\)
    \item tangent Fisher information and the share carried by the covariance block
          \cite{bessonFisherInformationMatrix2013}
    \item edge density of each precision graph
    \item separation: MixSim overlap, mean pairwise Hellinger distance, affine-invariant Riemannian distance
  \end{itemize}
  \item scores and their pitfalls: Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Optimisation}
\label{sec:optimisation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item objective: the Gaussian log-likelihood \eqref{eq-loglikelihood-multivariate-gaussian}; with
        \(\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{p})^{-1}\), its score is
\end{itemize}

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial p_{j}}
  =-p_{j}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})
  +\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{r}
  +p_{j}\,\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{r}
  \label{eq-derivative-log-likelihood-unconstrained}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item the three terms come from the log-determinant, the generalised least-squares residual and the
        composition-dependent weight; the root of the score has no closed form, so the maximum is
        approximated by iterative second-order methods
  \item the analytic Hessian is in Appendix~\ref{app:derivation}; both derivatives were checked against
        numerical derivatives with Richardson extrapolation \cite{R-numDeriv}
  \item keeping the factor \(\frac12\) of the Gaussian log-density on both terms of
        \eqref{eq-loglikelihood-multivariate-gaussian} makes the expected negative Hessian equal to the
        Fisher information used for the Wald intervals
  \item the score and Hessian above are \gls{ambient}; the solvers work on the \gls{ilr} \gls{chart}
        \(\boldsymbol{p}=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\rho})\) with a \gls{helmert} \(\mathbf{V}\), a \(C^{2}\) diffeomorphism of the
        open simplex \cite{egozcueIsometricLogratioTransformations2003}; every chart gives the same estimate and the
        same intervals, and ILR is the one whose numerics do not depend on a reference type
        (Appendix~\ref{sec:ilr-choice}); the additive log-ratio is kept as an alternative chart
  \item solvers: Newton--Raphson (default), with the analytic score and Hessian in ILR coordinates and the
        trust-region safeguards of \texttt{stats::nlminb}; L-BFGS-B on the closure
        \(\boldsymbol{p}=\boldsymbol{w}/\mathbf{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{w}\) of the box \(\boldsymbol{w}\ge\mathbf{0}\), which maximises the same
        likelihood, can return exact zeros, and whose stationary points are those of the simplex
        (Appendix~\ref{app:optim-solvers}); Marquardt--Levenberg (alternative), a damped Newton
        method (\texttt{marqLevAlg}) stopped on the relative distance to the maximum
        \cite{marquardtAlgorithmLeastSquaresEstimation1963,philippsRobustEfficientOptimization2021,commengesNewtonLikeAlgorithmLikelihood2006};
        \gls{fisherscoring} is not implemented (Section~\ref{sec:optim-perspective})
  \item the log-likelihood is not concave, also in the ILR chart; a converged solver certifies a
        local maximum at best, hence Dirichlet multistarts (Appendix~\ref{app:sim-init})
  \item convergence is reported as a small ILR score and a negative-definite Hessian; a large \gls{condnum}
        of that Hessian flags an anisotropic peak or a plateau (Appendix~\ref{app:mle-geometry}); other solvers
        and their links with generalised least squares are in Appendix~\ref{app:optim}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}
\label{sec:intervals}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Wald: the expected information \(I(\boldsymbol{p})\) (Appendix~\ref{app:derivation}) pulled back to ILR coordinates,
        \(I_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=\mathbf{J}_{\psi}^{\top}I(\boldsymbol{p})\mathbf{J}_{\psi}\), then the delta method to \(\boldsymbol{p}\)
        \cite{oehlertNoteDeltaMethod1992}; the interval is symmetric and can leave \([0,1]\) for a rare type, whereas
        intervals formed in ILR coordinates and mapped back respect the bounds
  \item likelihood ratio: the profile deviance with a \(\chi^{2}_{1}\) cut-off \cite{wilksLargeSampleDistributionLikelihood1938},
        invariant to the chart and inside the simplex; it also tests linear constraints between proportions;
        the score test uses the slope at the null value; the three intervals coincide only for a quadratic
        log-likelihood, so their disagreement diagnoses a non-Gaussian geometry (Appendices~\ref{app:mle-geometry}
        and~\ref{app:mle-tests})
  \item regularity: all three need an interior truth, a fixed number of parameters and a growing number of
        independent samples sharing one composition (Proposition~\ref{pr:mle-regularity}); for a single bulk
        sample, the single-cell model \eqref{eq:sc-model} offers instead a limit in the number of cells of every
        type, called small-dispersion asymptotics \cite{jørgensenSmallDispersionAsymptotics1987}
  \item boundary: an absent type puts the null on a face, where Wald fails and the likelihood ratio follows a
        chi-bar-square mixture, calibrated by a restricted parametric bootstrap; a reference bootstrap propagates
        the uncertainty of \((\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\})\) (Appendix~\ref{app:mle-boundary})
  \item one bulk column is an \(N=1\) fit: the interval describes that maximiser, and the
        maximum-likelihood bias is of order \(N^{-1}\)
  \item full argument: Appendix~\ref{app:mle} and the
        \vignette{theory-population-inference.html}{population inference vignette}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Single-cell references: DeCovarT as a mixture of independent cells}
\label{sec:sc-tailored}

\begin{itemize}
  \item setting: one UMI reference from one donor, one bulk sample at a time, no external covariate, no
        gene-wise scale estimated from other bulks, and no split of size factors into technical and
        biological parts
  \item the reference observes the cells themselves and the bulk is their physical sum, so independent cells add
        with their counts \(m_{ji}=n_{i}p_{ji}\): the covariance is linear in the composition, as in \emph{RNA-Sieve}
        and \emph{ReDeconv} \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021,luTranscriptomeSizeMatters2025}
  \item one donor identifies the within-donor covariance \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}\) only; the between-donor layer
        \(\boldsymbol{S}_{B}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})\) needs \(R\ge2\) donors \cite{zimmermanPracticalSolutionPseudoreplication2021}
        (Section~\ref{sec:generalized-linear-model})
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Generative model and core assumptions}
\label{sec:sc-generative}

\begin{itemize}
  \item reference moments, computed on raw UMI counts without library-size normalisation:
        \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}=C_{j}^{-1}\sum_{c\in j}\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}\), so that
        \(S_{j}=\mathbf{1}^{\top}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}\) is the mean transcriptome size of a type-\(j\) cell, and
        \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}\), the within-type cell-to-cell covariance of the expression
        \cite{sarkarSeparatingMeasurementExpression2021,fangExtrinsicBiologicalStochasticity2025}, estimated and
        regularised as in the paragraph below
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(B\arabic*)]
  \item the bulk is the physical sum of \(n_{i}\) cells on a linear scale, \(m_{ji}=n_{i}p_{ji}\) of type \(j\), times one
        sample-wide scale: \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}=a_{i}\sum_{j}\boldsymbol{T}_{ji}\)
  \item cells are independent draws within their type, with mean \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}\) and covariance
        \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}\); with no correlation between cells the bulk is a convolution of cells
  \item every \(m_{ji}\) is large, so the central limit theorem applies (\(n_{i}\) runs from thousands to millions)
  \item one donor and one sample-wide scale: no between-donor layer and no gene-wise platform ratio
  \item plug-in reference moments; one fit per bulk sample
\end{enumerate}

\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\mid\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}
    &\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}\Bigl(
      a_{i}n_{i}\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},\;
      a_{i}^{2}n_{i}\sum_{j}p_{ji}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}
    \Bigr),
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}),\\
    \text{equivalently}\quad
    \tilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_{\cdot i}
    &\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}\bigl(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},\;n_{i}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})\bigr)
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:sc-model}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item one model: the second line is the per-cell bulk \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_{\cdot i}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}/c_{i}\), the bulk divided by
        \(c_{i}=a_{i}n_{i}\);
        Appendix~\ref{app:sc-derivation} derives both lines step by step, exact for independent Gaussian cells and a
        central-limit approximation for counts; the per-cell form separates the composition from two orthogonal
        scales and keeps the information matrix well conditioned (condition number about \(10^{4}\)--\(10^{7}\), against
        up to \(10^{18}\) when the raw scales \(a_{i}\) and \(n_{i}\) are estimated)
  \item estimand: \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\) keeps the cell sizes \(S_{j}\), so \(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\) are cell fractions
        directly; transcript fractions follow as \(q_{ji}=p_{ji}S_{j}/\sum_{\ell}p_{\ell i}S_{\ell}\)
        \cite{wangBulkTissueCell2019,racleSimultaneousEnumerationCancer2017}; only the ratios \(S_{j}/S_{\ell}\) matter;
        Appendix~\ref{sec:estimand} gives two alternatives, returning transcript fractions or taking external cell sizes
  \item scales: \(a_{i}\) and \(n_{i}\) enter only through \(c_{i}=a_{i}n_{i}\) in the mean and
        \(\phi_{i}=a_{i}c_{i}=a_{i}^{2}n_{i}\) in the covariance, two free positive scalars estimated inside the
        likelihood\footnote{Three side remarks on the bulk scale and noise. (i) The scale \(c_{i}\) is a likelihood
        parameter; the bulk is never divided by its own total, which would be a library-size (TPM-type) closure with the
        problems of Section~\ref{sec:limitations} (Remark~\ref{rem:scale-not-closure}). (ii) No separate bulk measurement
        term is fitted: for UMI-like bulk counts a Poisson term is the diagonal of the mean and adds no parameter, whereas
        for read counts its dispersion is weakly identified from one sample (Appendix~\ref{app:which-covariance}).
        (iii) The model shares the multiplicative mean of the Poisson measurement models of \emph{RCTD}
        \cite{cableRobustDecompositionCell2022a} and of Sarkar and Stephens \cite{sarkarSeparatingMeasurementExpression2021}
        (Remark~\ref{rem:poisson-link}).}; as in \emph{RNA-Sieve}, the scale comes from a central-limit argument on a
        sum of \(n_{i}\) cells, its variance grows linearly in \(n_{i}\), and it is estimated, not supplied; RNA-Sieve's
        \(n\) is a number of cells only within one protocol \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021}, and
        here \(n_{i}\) is one only when \(a_{i}\) is known (same platform, or paired samples)
  \item moment reading of the scales: for a fixed composition, \(\hat c_{i}\) solves
        \(\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}-c_{i}\boldsymbol{m})=0\) with \(\boldsymbol{m}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\) and
        \(\boldsymbol{W}=\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})^{-1}\), a condition on the first moment, and \(\hat\phi_{i}=\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{r}/G\)
        matches the second moment of the whitened residual; two conditions for two scales make an exactly identified
        generalised method of moments, equal to the Gaussian likelihood profile and still consistent when only the first
        two moments are right, with the sandwich variance of Section~\ref{sec:moment-inference}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Estimating the within-type covariance.}
\label{sec:cov-estimation}


\begin{itemize}
  \item target: one precision \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j,t}\) per type and time point; with one specimen, the
        estimand is the within-type cell-to-cell covariance of the expression on the count scale, the raw
        covariance minus its Poisson sampling diagonal; whether the denoised or the raw covariance should enter the
        bulk depends on the sequencing depth per cell of the bulk (Appendix~\ref{app:which-covariance}); Pearson
        residuals serve gene selection (Section~\ref{sec:feature-selection}), not this plug-in
  \item five estimators, compared in the gastruloid ablation
  \begin{itemize}
    \item partial correlations by inversion with Benjamini--Hochberg edges and a positive-definite refit on the
          support \cite{R-ppcor}; valid only when \(n_{j}>G\)
    \item graphical lasso \cite{friedmanSparseInverseCovariance2008} on the correlation matrix, with an
          unpenalised diagonal and a per-type extended BIC \cite{foygelExtendedBayesianInformation2010}, then
          rescaled to the data
    \item de-sparsified graphical lasso on nonparanormal scores with global false discovery rate control
          \cite{jankovaConfidenceIntervalsHighdimensional2015,liuNonparanormalSemiparametricEstimation2009,liuGaussianGraphicalModel2013,zhangSILGGMExtensivePackage2018}
    \item Poisson--log-normal network on raw UMI counts with a library-size offset
          \cite{chiquetVariationalInferenceSparse2018}
    \item dense shrinkage of the correlations with the variances kept
          \cite{schäferShrinkageApproachLargescale2005}, judged by held-out Gaussian log-likelihood
  \end{itemize}
  \item sparsity is secondary for DeCovarT: the likelihood needs \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\), and the mixture covariance,
        \(\sum_{j}p_{j}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}\) here or \(\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) for population references, has no
        sparse precision even when every \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}\) is sparse
  \item from precision to covariance: an estimator that returns \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}\) (partial correlations, the
        de-sparsified graphical lasso) is inverted once per type, before any fit, through its Cholesky factor
        \cite{golubMatrixComputations2013}: the factorisation certifies that \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}\) is positive definite,
        the inverse is exactly symmetric, at least as accurate as a QR inverse and three to five times faster for
        \(G=200\) to \(800\); if the factorisation fails, the diagonal of \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}\) is shifted uniformly, which
        keeps the graph, until both factors exist; the graphical lasso
        returns \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\) directly; this \(O(G^{3})\) inversion is paid once, whereas the mixture covariance
        is factorised at every likelihood evaluation (Appendix~\ref{app:optim-structured})
  \item empty graphs are a detection limit: above \(\max_{g\neq g'}|R_{gg'}|\) the graphical lasso returns a
        diagonal precision, and recovery needs \(n\asymp d^{2}\log G\) for maximum degree \(d\)
        \cite{chiquetContributionsSparseMethods2015,heinävaaraInconsistencyℓ1penalisedSparse2016};
        the dense shrinkage estimate is the fallback plug-in
  \item review of the estimators and their conditions: Appendix~\ref{app:networks}
\end{itemize}


\subsubsection{In silico mixtures}
\label{sec:sc-synthetic}

\begin{itemize}
  \item the model is simulated as written, with the exact technical factors, so that the estimator is checked before
        any calibration on real data
  \item cell counts \(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n_{i},\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})\); type totals
        \(\boldsymbol{T}_{ji}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(m_{ji}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},m_{ji}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j})\), exact for a sum of
        \(m_{ji}\) independent Gaussian cells; bulk \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}=a_{i}\sum_{j}\boldsymbol{T}_{ji}\); the target is the
        realised fraction \(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}/n_{i}\)
  \item reference moments reuse the generators of Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}: a cell of size \(S_{j}\) has mean
        \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}=S_{j}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\cdot j}\) and covariance \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}=S_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\theta}_{j}\), since a cell
        \(S_{j}\) times larger is \(S_{j}\) times larger in every gene; the descriptors of Table~\ref{tab:descriptors} are
        reported with the size ratio \(\max_{j}S_{j}/\min_{j}S_{j}\)
  \item two designs use this generator
  \begin{itemize}
    \item calibration of the intervals: \(G=10\), \(J=2\), composition \((0.5,0.5)\) or \((0.8,0.2)\), sizes \((1,1)\) or
          \((1,5)\), \(n_{i}=10^{4}\) cells, \(a_{i}=1\), 300 replicates per scenario
          (\repo{blob/main/scripts/benchmark_metrics/check_confidence_interval_coverage.R}{coverage script}); the
          solver comparison of Appendix~\ref{sec:sc-newton} adds \(a_{i}=2\)
    \item the benchmark with two lineages and the competing methods (Section~\ref{sec:sc-hierarchical-design})
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Optimisation}
\label{sec:sc-optimisation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item for a fixed composition, the scales have closed forms: the generalised least-squares estimate
        \(\hat c(\boldsymbol{p})=(\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{m})^{-1}\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{y}\) and the Mahalanobis
        estimate \(\hat\phi(\boldsymbol{p})=\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{r}/G\); substituting them gives the profile log-likelihood
        \(\ell^{\ast}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\) of the \(J-1\) ILR coordinates, and Newton--Raphson runs on \(\ell^{\ast}\) with its exact score and
        Hessian (Appendix~\ref{sec:sc-newton})
  \item one loop: the weight matrix \(\boldsymbol{W}=\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p})^{-1}\) depends on the unknown composition, and the profile
        rebuilds it, together with \(\hat c\) and \(\hat\phi\), at every trial composition; the fit is a feasible generalised least
        squares without an outer loop, and its limit is the joint maximum-likelihood estimate of \((\boldsymbol{p},c,\phi)\)
        (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sc-two-stage}); \emph{MuSiC} \cite{wangBulkTissueCell2019} and \emph{deMixSC}
        \cite{guoDeMixSCDeconvolutionFramework2023} iterate gene weights in the same spirit, from residual and cross-subject variances or
        from the squared fitted value and squared residual, whereas DeCovarT takes them from the model covariance at the current
        composition; a robust variant down-weights genes with large residuals (Appendix~\ref{sec:robust})
  \item curvature and step: the observed Hessian of \(\ell^{\ast}\), safeguarded by the trust region of \texttt{stats::nlminb}
        \cite{R-base}, which shrinks or enlarges the step with the agreement between the predicted and the actual increase
        (Remark~\ref{rem:step-size}); \gls{fisherscoring} with step-halving is not used, because the trust region already handles an
        indefinite Hessian and, with \(c^{2}/\phi=n\) cells, the observed and expected information nearly coincide at the maximum
  \item the start solves the quadratic programme of \emph{DeconRNASeq} \cite{gongDeconRNASeqStatisticalFramework2013} with a free
        scale (Appendix~\ref{sec:sc-newton})
  \item alternative optimiser: L-BFGS-B on the closure chart of Section~\ref{sec:optimisation}, applied to the same profile
        log-likelihood; it can return exact zeros, and the parametric bootstrap refits with the optimiser that produced the
        estimate
  \item a two-stage variant keeps the scales at their start values; it is not the joint maximum, and is kept for comparison
        only
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Inference}
\label{sec:sc-inference}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Wald intervals use the Schur complement of the information, so estimating \(c_{i}\) and \(\phi_{i}\) widens them; the negative
        Hessian of the profile \(\ell^{\ast}\) is the observed counterpart of that Schur complement, so the profile fit carries the
        correction for the estimated scales, whereas the two-stage variant needs the plug-in correction of
        Appendix~\ref{app:sc-inference}
  \item few genes: \(\hat\phi_{i}\) has \(\nu=G-J\) residual degrees of freedom, and its maximum-likelihood divisor \(G\) biases it
        downwards by about \(\nu/G\); with \(G=10\) and \(J=2\), the 95\% Wald, ILR and profile intervals covered the realised
        fractions 84--93\% of the time, and those for \(c_{i}\) and \(\phi_{i}\) 82--90\% (calibration design of
        Section~\ref{sec:sc-synthetic}); the REML calibration inflates the covariances by \(G/\nu\), takes quantiles from
        \(t_{\nu}\), gives \(\phi_{i}\) the interval \([G\hat\phi_{i}/\chi^{2}_{\nu;1-\alpha/2},\,G\hat\phi_{i}/\chi^{2}_{\nu;\alpha/2}]\) and
        the profile interval the cut-off \(G\log\{1+F_{1,\nu;1-\alpha}/\nu\}\), exact in a linear model with unknown variance;
        it brought the coverage to 93--97\% for the fractions and 94--96\% for the scales

  \item profile intervals concentrate both scales out (Appendices~\ref{app:sc-inference} and~\ref{app:mle-nuisance});
        a single bulk sample has the small-dispersion limit in the number of cells
        \cite{jørgensenSmallDispersionAsymptotics1987}; boundary tests and bootstraps are those of
        Section~\ref{sec:inference}
  \item other alignment strategies and why they were not chosen: Appendix~\ref{app:alignment}
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
  \caption{Profile Newton fit of \eqref{eq:sc-model} for one bulk sample: the two nuisance scales are concentrated out in
    closed form at every composition, and a trust-region Newton method maximises the profile log-likelihood of the cell
    fractions.}
  \label{alg:sc-two-stage}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require bulk \(\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{G}\); cell sizes \(S_{j}\), estimated from the reference or supplied
      externally (Appendix~\ref{sec:estimand}); reference means \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}=S_{j}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\cdot j}\);
      regularised \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,J}\); Helmert basis \(\mathbf{V}\); tolerance \(\varepsilon\); \(G>J\)
    \Statex \textit{Start}
    \State \((c^{(0)},\boldsymbol{p}^{(0)})\gets\arg\min_{c>0,\,\boldsymbol{p}\in\Delta^{J-1}}\lVert\boldsymbol{y}-c\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{p}\rVert_{2}^{2}\);
      \(\boldsymbol{p}^{(0)}\) floored away from zero
      \Comment{quadratic programme of \emph{DeconRNASeq}}
    \State \(\boldsymbol{\rho}\gets\mathbf{V}^{\top}\log\boldsymbol{p}^{(0)}\); trust radius \(\Delta\gets1\)
    \Statex \textit{Profile Newton iterations}
    \Repeat
      \State \(\boldsymbol{p}\gets\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\rho})\);
        \(\boldsymbol{W}\gets\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p})^{-1}\);
        \(\boldsymbol{m}\gets\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{p}\)
      \State \(\hat c\gets(\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{m})^{-1}\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{y}\);
        \(\boldsymbol{r}\gets\boldsymbol{y}-\hat c\,\boldsymbol{m}\);
        \(\hat\phi\gets\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{r}/G\)
        \Comment{scales rebuilt at every iterate (Remark~\ref{rem:scale-not-closure})}
      \State \(U_{j}\gets\hat\phi^{-1}\bigl(\hat c\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{r}
        +\tfrac12\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{r}\bigr)
        -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j})\)
        \Comment{profile score, by the \gls{envelope}}
      \State \(\boldsymbol{H}^{\ast}\gets\boldsymbol{H}_{pp}-\boldsymbol{H}_{p\eta}\boldsymbol{H}_{\eta\eta}^{-1}\boldsymbol{H}_{\eta p}\), with \(\boldsymbol{\eta}=(c,\phi)\)
        \Comment{profile Hessian \eqref{eq:sc-profile-hessian}}
      \State \(\boldsymbol{U}_{\rho}\gets\mathbf{J}_{\psi}^{\top}\boldsymbol{U}\);
        \(\boldsymbol{H}_{\rho}\gets\mathbf{J}_{\psi}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{\ast}\mathbf{J}_{\psi}+\sum_{j}U_{j}\nabla^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}p_{j}\)
      \State \(\boldsymbol{\delta}\gets(\lambda\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\rho})^{-1}\boldsymbol{U}_{\rho}\), with the smallest \(\lambda\ge0\) such that
        \(\lambda\mathbf{I}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\rho}\) is \gls{posdef} and \(\lVert\boldsymbol{\delta}\rVert_{2}\le\Delta\)
        \Comment{trust-region step (Remark~\ref{rem:step-size})}
      \State compare the actual increase \(\ell^{\ast}(\boldsymbol{\rho}+\boldsymbol{\delta})-\ell^{\ast}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\) with the predicted
        increase \(\boldsymbol{U}_{\rho}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}+\tfrac12\boldsymbol{\delta}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}_{\rho}\boldsymbol{\delta}\): accept
        \(\boldsymbol{\rho}\gets\boldsymbol{\rho}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\) when it is a non-trivial fraction, then shrink \(\Delta\) after a poor
        prediction and enlarge it after a good one; otherwise shrink \(\Delta\) and recompute \(\boldsymbol{\delta}\)
    \Until{the relative change of \(\ell^{\ast}\) or of \(\boldsymbol{\rho}\) over a Newton step is below \(\varepsilon\)}
    \State \(\hat c,\hat\phi\gets\) closed forms at \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\); \(\hat a\gets\hat\phi/\hat c\), \(\hat n\gets\hat c^{2}/\hat\phi\)
      \Comment{\(\hat n\) is a number of cells only when \(a\) is known}
    \State \(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}})\gets\mathbf{J}_{\psi}(-\boldsymbol{H}_{\rho})^{-1}\mathbf{J}_{\psi}^{\top}\) at \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\)
      \Comment{returned as \texttt{vcov}}
    \Ensure cell fractions \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\), transcript fractions \(\hat q_{j}=\hat p_{j}S_{j}/\sum_{\ell}\hat p_{\ell}S_{\ell}\),
      scales \(\hat c,\hat\phi,\hat a,\hat n\), and \(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}})\)
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Synthetic hierarchical scenario}
\label{sec:sc-hierarchical-design}

\begin{itemize}
  \item four cell types in two lineages, types 1--2 and types 3--4, simulated by the generator of
        Section~\ref{sec:sc-synthetic}; the target is the realised fraction \(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}/n_{i}\); since the size
        multiplies the covariance by \(S_{j}^{2}\), the overlap between two types of equal size does not depend on that size
  \item mean signature: target cosines \(0.8\) within a lineage and \(0.3\) between lineages, built with non-negative
        means (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}); every gene is expressed in all four types, and the expression of a gene in
        the other lineage is about \(13\%\) of its main level (\(4\%\) for a cosine of \(0.1\), close to disjoint marker
        blocks)
  \item precision graphs: one skeleton per lineage, shared exactly by its two siblings, a stochastic block model with
        three blocks of equal size for types 1--2 \cite{hollandStochasticBlockmodelsFirst1983} and a scale-free graph for
        types 3--4 \cite{barabasiEmergenceScalingRandom1999}; the siblings share the signed weights of their lineage except
        for \(5\%\) of the edges, whose sign each flips independently; with independent signs the sibling covariances
        differ so much at \(G=200\) that a sibling overlap of \(0.08\) cannot be reached (at most \(0.013\)); spectral
        shift as in Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}
  \item one covariance scale per number of genes and overlap level, chosen so that the mean overlap of the two sibling
        pairs is \(0.01\) or \(0.08\) at the balanced composition (realised means \(0.009\) to \(0.081\)); the two pairs
        differ, from \(0.0005\) to \(0.149\), because the block model and the scale-free graph give the two lineages
        different covariances; between-lineage overlaps stay at or below \(0.001\), and the sibling overlaps are
        identical for equal and unequal cell sizes
  \item crossed factors, two levels each, \(2^{6}=64\) scenarios, the five factors below and the platform scale \(a=1\) or \(5\)
  \begin{itemize}
    \item composition: balanced \((1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\), or unbalanced within each lineage \((0.4,0.1,0.4,0.1)\)
    \item sibling overlap: \(0.01\) or \(0.08\)
    \item cells per bulk: \(n_{i}=10^{3}\) or \(10^{5}\), so that the rarest type counts at least about \(100\) cells
    \item cell sizes: equal, or lineage 3--4 five times larger, \(\boldsymbol{S}=(1,1,5,5)\)
    \item genes: \(G=20\) or \(200\); the maximum-likelihood \(\hat\phi\) is biased down by about \((G-J)/G\), \(0.8\)
          and \(0.98\) here
  \end{itemize}
  \item platform scale: rescaling the bulk by \(a\) rescales \(\hat c\) by \(a\) and \(\hat\phi\) by \(a^{2}\) and leaves
        \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\) unchanged (the profile log-likelihood only shifts by \(-G\log a\)), so the \(a=1\) half
        is redundant for DeCovarT and checks that invariance numerically; it also tests the assumption of
        \emph{RNA-Sieve}, whose mean and variance share one scale
  \item estimand and reference: cell fractions, with the cell sizes of the generator and the raw covariances
        (\texttt{cellular\_raw}, Appendix~\ref{sec:estimand})
  \item caveats: the cells are Gaussian, so a sum of cells is exactly Gaussian at any \(n_{i}\) and the central-limit
        argument of (B3) is not itself tested, and \(20\) to \(30\%\) of the simulated single-cell values are negative,
        which the reference panels of \emph{MEAD} and \emph{RNA-Sieve} receive as such; the per-cell overlap describes
        the reference, whereas the precision of a bulk also grows with \(n_{i}\), which the Wald standard error reported
        with each scenario captures
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Which genes enter the signature}
\label{sec:feature-selection}

\begin{itemize}
  \item aim: genes that separate close types and that have a non-degenerate distribution in every type
  \item presence gate, fixed before ranking: detection rate \(\pi_{gj}\ge0.05\) and within-type variance
        \(v_{gj}>0\) in every type; a constant gene makes \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) singular
  \item main effect of cell type, tested against the technical null
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \(M_{0}:\log\mu_{cg}=\beta_{0g}+\log L_{c}\), \(y_{cg}\sim\mathrm{NB}(\mu_{cg},\theta_{g})\), with library size
          \(L_{c}\) \cite{hafemeisterNormalizationVarianceStabilization2019}; \(M_{1}\) adds the type effect, a joint
          test on \(J-1\) degrees of freedom \cite{agrestiFoundationsLinearGeneralized2015}
    \item likelihood-ratio statistic \(D_{g}(M_{0})-D_{g}(M_{1})\); the Wald test is avoided because an all-zero
          type sends \(\hat\beta_{gj}\to-\infty\) (Hauck--Donner effect) \cite{yeeHauckDonnerEffectWald2022}
    \item the between-type sum of squares of Pearson residuals under \(M_{0}\) is, up to weight heterogeneity,
          the Rao score statistic of this test; divided by the within-type dispersion it is a quasi-likelihood
          \(F\) test \cite{lundDetectingDifferentialExpression2012,glmGamPoi2020}
    \item overdispersed genes are kept: their within-type scatter is the covariance DeCovarT models
  \end{itemize}
  \item calibration and stability
  \begin{itemize}
    \item the nominal \(F\) tail is optimistic on clipped, discrete residuals
          \cite{lauseAnalyticPearsonResiduals2021}; the null is calibrated by permuting cell-type labels,
          with Freedman--Lane residual permutation when covariates are present
          \cite{freedmanNonstochasticInterpretation1983}
    \item a bootstrap of cells within type measures how often a gene stays in the panel
          \cite{meinshausenStabilitySelection2010}; it does not generate a null
  \end{itemize}
  \item ranking: with thousands of cells nearly every expressed gene rejects the null, so the test is a gate
        and genes are ranked by effect size (between-type share of variance, deviance explained, or the
        smallest pairwise Hellinger distance between type-specific laws)
  \item the \(p\)-values calibrate a screen inside one specimen, not population-level differential expression
  \item details: feature-selection chapter of the reproducibility book
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\begin{itemize}
  \item primary accuracy scores on the simplex: total variation \(\tfrac12\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}-\boldsymbol{p}^{\star}\rVert_{1}\),
        RMSE and the maximum absolute error; all are finite with zeros, but abundant types dominate them
  \item compositional scores (Aitchison distance) measure fold-change error and weigh rare types fairly, but are
        undefined at zero; they are reported only on interior estimates, with the fraction of zeros
        \cite{aitchisonStatisticalAnalysisCompositional1982}
  \item Monte Carlo summaries: bias, empirical standard deviation, the ratio of model standard error to
        empirical standard deviation, coverage with its Wilson interval \cite{wilsonProbableInferenceLaw1927},
        and mean interval width
  \item optimisation: numerical and theoretical convergence, and the projected-score residual
  \item formulas, pitfalls and the recommended table: Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and Perspectives}
\label{sec:perspectives}

\subsection{DeCovarT's core innovations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item DeCovarT collects, in one likelihood, ideas from algorithms that model within-type variability
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{MuSiC}: one scale aligns the bulk average with the single-cell average, for library size and
          capture efficiency across platforms \cite{wangBulkTissueCell2019}
    \item \emph{RNA-Sieve}: Gaussian cell-type profiles justified by a central-limit argument on the moments,
          with independent genes; DeCovarT drops gene independence; \emph{RNA-Sieve} reports Wald regions from the
          inverse Fisher information of its gene-wise likelihood, and a Godambe sandwich when a protocol shift takes
          the truth outside the model family \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021}; its released code
          returns \([0,1]\) when the averaged Hessian is ill-conditioned, so the benchmarks recompute both intervals
          (Section~\ref{sec:hd});
          DeCovarT's analogue of the first is Remark~\ref{pr:decovart-expected-fisher}, which is not calibrated on a
          simplex face, where the chi-bar-square likelihood-ratio test and a restricted parametric bootstrap are
          required; the noisy-mean term of \emph{RNA-Sieve} is the likelihood analogue of resampling the reference,
          whereas DeCovarT keeps \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) plug-in and propagates their uncertainty by a donor- or
          cell-level bootstrap
    \item \emph{DECALS}: composition and cell-type covariances estimated together, so within-type and
          cross-sample variability both enter the fit \cite{caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024}
    \item \emph{MEAD}: an errors-in-variables Gaussian model with confidence intervals for the proportions
          \cite{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026}
  \end{itemize}
  \item benchmarks that cross algorithms with normalisation choices use raw counts as the proxy of mRNA
        abundance; library-size normalisations turn the problem into a composition on the gene simplex; the
        sample scale of \eqref{eq:sc-model} is estimated inside the likelihood and is not such a normalisation
        (Remark~\ref{rem:scale-not-closure})
  \item the scaling assumptions of Figure~\ref{fig:overview} are unlikely to hold; absolute mRNA content per
        type then needs an external reference or an orthogonal assay
  \item estimand: with population-level references, the share of each type on the scale of the reference profiles;
        with a single-cell reference, cell fractions when the cell sizes \(S_{j}\) are kept in the reference means, and
        transcript fractions otherwise (Appendix~\ref{sec:estimand}); never absolute cell counts
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Modelling: replicated references and several bulk samples}
\label{sec:generalized-linear-model}
\label{sec:mean-variance}

The fitted model \eqref{eq:sc-model} uses one reference donor and one bulk sample at a time. Replication on
either side identifies variance terms that one sample cannot separate.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \(R\ge2\) single-cell replicates separate three layers
        (\vignette{theory-single-cell-generative-optimisation.html\#sec-reference}{single-cell generative and optimisation vignette}):
        the within-donor cell-to-cell covariance, linear in the composition because independent cells add;
        the between-donor biological covariance, quadratic because one population profile per type is shared
        by all its cells; and the diagonal measurement noise \(\boldsymbol{D}_{i}\)
        \cite{zimmermanPracticalSolutionPseudoreplication2021}
        \begin{equation}
          \boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\mid\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}\Bigl(c_{i}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},\;
          c_{i}^{2}\sum_{j}p_{ji}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{B,j}+\phi_{i}\sum_{j}p_{ji}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}+\boldsymbol{D}_{i}\Bigr)
          \label{eq:four-layer}
        \end{equation}
        the covariance derivative \(2c_{i}^{2}p_{j}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{B,j}+\phi_{i}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{W,j}\) mixes both weights; the
        population-level model keeps the quadratic layer alone and the single-cell model the linear layer alone
  \item \(N\) bulk samples identify the measurement term: with one sample, a Poisson diagonal
        \(\omega\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{m}_{i})\) or its negative-binomial extension cannot be separated from \(\phi_{i}\) and from
        the cell-to-cell covariance; across samples, if the technical scales are orthogonal to the composition
        (Appendix~\ref{app:mle-nuisance}) and the normalisation scale is shared, residuals pooled over samples
        estimate a gene-wise mean--variance relationship, \(\operatorname{Var}(y_{gi})=\omega m_{gi}+\kappa_{g}m_{gi}^{2}\); a Poisson
        variance shrinks relative to the mean as \(n_{i}\) grows, whereas a quadratic term does not and keeps
        the intervals from narrowing with depth (Appendix~\ref{app:mle-regularity})
  \item generalised linear model: DeCovarT already models the mean through an inverse link on the parameter,
        \(\boldsymbol{m}(\boldsymbol{\rho})=c\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\), instead of transforming the response, which would change
        the estimand and break the additive mixture \cite{agrestiFoundationsLinearGeneralized2015}; with an explicit
        law for \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\) (Gaussian, Poisson, negative binomial), the variance function above completes a
        generalised linear model; transformation-based methods such as \emph{DeOPUS} fix the gene weights in
        advance and have no likelihood \cite{nguyenDeOPUSCellularDeconvolution2026}
  \item quasi-likelihood: without an explicit law, the first two moments
        \(\mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{y}\mid\boldsymbol{\rho})=\boldsymbol{m}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(\boldsymbol{y}\mid\boldsymbol{\rho})=\phi\boldsymbol{V}_{0}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\) define the
        quasi-score \(\boldsymbol{D}^{\top}\boldsymbol{V}_{0}^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{m}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\}=\boldsymbol{0}\), with
        \(\boldsymbol{D}=c\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mathbf{J}_{\psi}\); it is unbiased whenever the mean is right, its information
        \(\phi^{-1}\boldsymbol{D}^{\top}\boldsymbol{V}_{0}^{-1}\boldsymbol{D}\) is the mean part of the Fisher information, and the dispersion follows
        from the Pearson statistic on \(G-J\) degrees of freedom
        (\vignette{theory-population-inference.html\#sec-decovart-glm}{GLM view})
  \item composition model across samples: \(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}=\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{z}_{i}+\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\), with \(\boldsymbol{z}_{i}\) a design row (time
        point, condition, batch), \(\boldsymbol{B}\) a \((J-1)\times K\) coefficient matrix and \(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\) a sample effect;
        shared technical scales are then estimated jointly over samples, and contrasts on \(\boldsymbol{B}\) test
        differential abundance; \emph{MEAD} regresses \(\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}=h(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0}+\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{f}_{i})\) by estimating
        equations, valid when \(N=o(G)\), with a coverage of group differences falling from 0.93 with 86 samples to
        0.81 with 1000 \cite{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026}; regressing ILR coordinates linearly on \(\boldsymbol{z}_{i}\)
        targets \(\mathbb{E}(\operatorname{ilr}\boldsymbol{p})\), not \(\operatorname{ilr}\mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{p})\); sample-level covariates may also modulate
        the cell-state moments \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}(\boldsymbol{z}_{i})\), as \emph{MuSiC2} does by removing condition-specific genes
        \cite{fanMusic2CellTypeDeconvolution2022}
  \item[] \todo[inline]{Implement the design-based fit \(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}=\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{z}_{i}+\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\) and evaluate the
        coverage of contrasts on \(\boldsymbol{B}\) in simulation, as a function of \(N/G\).}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Optimisation: Fisher scoring and iteratively reweighted least squares}
\label{sec:optim-perspective}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \gls{fisherscoring} replaces the observed Hessian by the expected information; its mean part is
        \gls{irls}: each step is a generalised least-squares regression of a working response on the current
        design \(\boldsymbol{D}\), weighted by the inverse covariance at the current composition, as for generalised linear
        models \cite[Sect.~4.5.4]{agrestiFoundationsLinearGeneralized2015} \cite{nelderGeneralizedLinearModels1972}
  \item the expected information is positive definite where the observed Hessian of the non-concave
        log-likelihood can be indefinite, so each Fisher-scoring step is an ascent direction; Newton--Raphson
        converges faster near the maximum; the two coincide when the covariance does not depend on the
        composition, the analogue of a canonical link (Appendix~\ref{app:irls})
  \item the same iteration fits the quasi-likelihood model of Section~\ref{sec:generalized-linear-model} and
        robust M-estimates with bounded gene weights (Appendix~\ref{sec:robust})
  \item block-coordinate alternative for the single-cell model, alternating the closed-form scales and the composition
        as \emph{deMixSC} alternates gene weights and proportions \cite{guoDeMixSCDeconvolutionFramework2023}: it reaches
        the joint maximum, but only at a linear rate because the scales are not orthogonal to the composition, which is why
        the implemented fit profiles the scales out (Section~\ref{sec:sc-optimisation} and Appendix~\ref{sec:sc-newton})
  \item first-order and stochastic methods (mini-batch gradients, adaptive steps) pay off only when many bulk
        samples share parameters (Appendix~\ref{app:optim-other})
  \item faster factorisations: every likelihood evaluation factorises the \(G\times G\) mixture covariance at a cost
        \(O(G^{3})\); when the cell-type covariances share a block, band or diagonal-plus-low-rank structure, the mixture
        inherits it and can be factorised faster, with the same likelihood, estimate and intervals
        (Appendix~\ref{app:optim-structured}); a speed-up for large \(G\), with no statistical effect
  \item[] \todo[inline]{Implement Fisher scoring in \texttt{fit\_decovart()}, switching to Newton--Raphson near the
        maximum, and compare iterations, failures and run time with the current solvers.}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Inference from estimating equations: GEE, GMM and finite-sample sandwich corrections}
\label{sec:moment-inference}

These corrections change the standard errors, not the estimate, so they belong to the inferential step.

\begin{itemize}
  \item the quasi-score is an unbiased estimating equation; \gls{gee} solve such equations for correlated
        responses with a working covariance, and they are the exactly identified case of the \gls{gmm}, which can
        also stack more moment conditions than parameters \cite{hansenLargeSampleProperties1982}, for example the mean and covariance equations of the
        bulk, and weight them optimally
  \item both keep the estimate consistent when the working covariance is wrong and report the \gls{sandwich};
        the sandwich needs independent units, the bulk samples, whereas the genes of one sample are correlated
        by construction, so with one bulk sample it needs a model of the gene--gene dependence
  \item that dependence is not known in practice; \emph{MEAD} estimates a set of dependent gene pairs by multiple
        testing on the bulk cohort, or from public data, and \emph{DECALS} estimates cell-type-specific covariances
        from the bulk cohort and iterates them with the covariance of the proportions
        \cite{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026,caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024}; DeCovarT already carries a
        dependence model, the supports of the precisions \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}\), which could replace that testing step
        (our proposal)
  \item finite samples: the plug-in sandwich underestimates the variance, because fitted residuals are smaller
        than the errors and correlated genes shrink the effective sample size; \gls{hccorr} inflate the
        residuals by their leverage, HC3 being the jackknife version
        \cite{mackinnonJackKniefeEstimator1985} that works best for \(N\le250\) \cite{longUsingHeteroscedasticityConsistent2000}; \emph{MEAD} applies HC3 as a leave-one-gene-out jackknife, notes that it fails for
        correlated genes, and replaces it by a jackknife that leaves out clusters of correlated genes
        \cite[Sect.~6.3]{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026}; \emph{DECALS} keeps the sandwich form of a least-squares
        variance but fills its meat with the model covariance \(\sum_{j}\hat p_{ji}^{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\) rather than with
        empirical outer products, with a finite-sample correction of the plugged-in squared proportions, and its
        authors attribute the under-coverage of \emph{MEAD} to an inconsistent empirical sandwich
        \cite{caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024}
  \item even with a corrected covariance, normal quantiles are too narrow when the variance estimate is itself
        noisy; Satterthwaite or Kenward--Roger degrees of freedom replace them by \(t\) or \(F\) quantiles
  \item for DeCovarT: the gene-factorised sandwich of Appendix~\ref{app:mle-firth}, with leverages from the
        generalised hat matrix of Appendix~\ref{app:irls}, and a jackknife over clusters of the precision graph
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Other observation laws allow correlation between genes}
\label{sec:outlook}

\begin{itemize}
  \item with few profiles, a logit-normal law can give better-calibrated intervals
        \cite{mcgregorProportionalitybasedAssociationMetrics2026}
  \item multivariate counts rarely have a closed form (negative-binomial or log-normal margins), so a
        convolution under those laws needs numerical integration or Monte Carlo
  \item alternative laws already used for deconvolution
  \begin{itemize}
    \item discrete counts: \emph{ISOpureR}, \emph{BayesPrism} (multinomial)
          \cite{anghelISOpureRImplementationComputational2015,chuCellTypeGeneExpression2022};
          \emph{DeconV} (Poisson) \cite{gynterDeconvProbabilisticCellType2023};
          Poisson--log-normal and zero-inflated extensions
          \cite{chiquetPoissonLognormalModelVersatile2021,batardiereZeroInflationMultivariatePoisson2025};
          \emph{RCTD} \cite{cableRobustDecompositionCell2022a}
    \item continuous, gene-wise: \emph{BLADE} \cite{andrade-barbosaBayesianLogNormalDeconvolution2021};
          \emph{bMIND} \cite{wangBayesianEstimationCelltypespecific2020}
  \end{itemize}
  \item \emph{CSNet} reverses \eqref{eq:convolution}: \(\boldsymbol{p}\) treated as known, type-level means and a
        sparse correlation estimated by least squares \cite{suEstimatingCellTypeSpecificGene2024}; its output is
        a co-expression graph, and a graphical lasso on \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\) gives the precision
        DeCovarT can plug in
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Deconvolution in a latent space: factor models and foundation-model embeddings}
\label{sec:latent-space}

DeCovarT deconvolves in the original gene space.
An alternative projects the bulk profiles and the reference into a lower-dimensional latent space
and solves the mixture there.

\begin{itemize}
  \item linear latent factor models keep the mixture linear, because a linear projection maps
        \(\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}\) to \(\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{y}=(\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{\mu})\boldsymbol{p}\)
  \begin{itemize}
    \item non-negative matrix factorisation writes the bulk matrix as a product of non-negative profiles
          and proportions, reference-free or guided by markers
          \cite{tangNITUMIDNonnegativeMatrix2020,hoyerNonnegativeMatrixFactorization2004}; NMF is ill-posed, and
          \emph{GSNMF+} restores the solvability conditions by augmenting the bulk with pseudo-bulks simulated
          from single-cell data, then corrects the remaining rescaling of the solution
          \cite{liAugmentedGSNMFModel2025}
    \item convex analysis of mixtures uses the geometry of the problem: bulk samples lie in the convex
          hull of the subtype profiles, the vertices identify marker genes, and minimum description length
          sets the number of subtypes \cite{chenCAMTHCConvexAnalysis2019}
    \item \emph{swCAM} adds sample-wise deviations \(\Delta\boldsymbol{S}_{i}\) to the subtype profiles, penalised by a
          nuclear norm and solved by ADMM, to recover subtype-specific expression in each sample; recovery is
          good for abundant subtypes and poor for rare ones (estimate--truth correlation from 0.20 to 0.71
          across abundance quartiles in its realistic simulation) \cite{chenSwCAMEstimationSubtypespecific2022}
  \end{itemize}
  \item non-linear embeddings learn the latent space
  \begin{itemize}
    \item deep networks trained on simulated pseudo-bulks predict proportions directly
          \cite{mendenDeepLearningBased2020}
    \item \emph{DECONVersation} embeds each bulk sample, treated as a pseudo-cell, and each cell-type signature
          with single-cell foundation models (Geneformer, Cell2Sentence, scGPT, CellHermes), then runs
          non-negative least squares on the embeddings \cite{okuDECONVersationSingleCell2026}; on six bulk datasets with ground
          truth it matched \emph{DWLS} and outperformed \emph{MuSiC} and \emph{BayesPrism}, and its advantage grew
          with the technical dissimilarity between reference and bulk
  \end{itemize}
  \item advantages of a latent space
  \begin{itemize}
    \item robustness: embeddings trained on large and heterogeneous corpora are less sensitive to batch and
          platform effects, and rank-based tokenisation acts as a normalisation
    \item no marker-selection step, whose choice changes the estimates; the signature is also better
          conditioned in embedding space than on raw genes
    \item gene interactions: mean-only methods ignore them, and DeCovarT captures only linear dependence
          through a Gaussian graphical model; deep models can encode non-linear, higher-order interactions
          between genes
  \end{itemize}
  \item drawbacks
  \begin{itemize}
    \item interpretation: latent coordinates carry no direct biological meaning, and attention-based marker
          enrichment is an indirect check
    \item the linear relationship between bulk and single-cell expression breaks: for a non-linear map
          \(f\), \(f(\sum_{j}p_{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j})\neq\sum_{j}p_{j}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j})\), so the proportions are no longer
          the parameters of a generative model; \emph{DECONVersation} checks additivity only by a cosine
          similarity that anisotropy inflates, and bulk samples are out of distribution for models trained on
          single cells
    \item inference: without a likelihood there is no finite-sample or asymptotic interval for
          \(\boldsymbol{p}\), no boundary test for an absent type, and no marginal effect of explanatory covariates
          (Section~\ref{sec:generalized-linear-model}); resampling remains, at a high computational cost
    \item cost and variability: fine-tuning needs a GPU, and results change with the chosen model
  \end{itemize}
  \item a bridge we suggest: a fixed linear projection \(\boldsymbol{L}\), such as factor loadings, keeps DeCovarT exact
        on \(\boldsymbol{y}^{\star}=\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{y}\), \(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\star}=\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{\mu}\) and
        \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}^{\star}=\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\boldsymbol{L}^{\top}\), so a low-dimensional covariance-driven fit keeps its
        likelihood and intervals; non-linear embeddings are then better used for gene or feature selection
        than as the space of the likelihood
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{A Bayesian framework: the Wiener filter}
\label{sec:wiener}

\begin{itemize}
  \item keeping the profiles as latent variables, with a diagonal residual \(\boldsymbol{D}\),
        \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\mid\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},\{\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j,i}\}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\sum_{j}p_{ji}\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j,i},\boldsymbol{D})\),
        returns the DeCovarT likelihood after integration; \(\boldsymbol{D}\) is a nugget variance, the geostatistical term for
        variation left at zero distance: here measurement error of the bulk, independent across genes and not explained
        by the cell-type profiles; \(\boldsymbol{D}\neq\boldsymbol{0}\) is needed for a non-degenerate posterior, because without
        it the bulk is an exact linear function of the latent profiles
  \item given \(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}\) the maximum a posteriori estimate, the best linear unbiased predictor
        and the Wiener filter coincide:
        \(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\cdot j,i}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}+p_{ji}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})+\boldsymbol{D}\}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}-\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})\);
        the cell-type covariance then shapes the purified profiles as well as \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\)
  \item the posterior covariance gives per-sample uncertainty on cell-type-specific profiles, which
        \emph{bMIND} obtains with a gene-wise single-cell prior \cite{wangBayesianEstimationCelltypespecific2020}
  \item the latent form yields an EM algorithm \cite{dempsterMaximumLikelihoodIncomplete1977} whose iterations
        increase the marginal likelihood, and a mean-field variant that keeps the sparse precisions
  \item single-cell references (Section~\ref{sec:sc-tailored}): the latent variable belongs to the cells, not to the genes;
        with the cell counts \(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n_{i},\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i})\) as missing data, the
        complete-data maximiser is \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\cdot i}=\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}/n_{i}\), on the simplex by construction, and
        \(c_{i}\) and \(\phi_{i}\) keep their closed forms; the E-step needs \(\mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}\mid\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i})\),
        a Gaussian update of the multinomial moments under the central limit theorem of (B3), approximate because the
        covariance of \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\mid\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}\) is linear in \(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i}\); a variational EM with a
        Gaussian \(q(\boldsymbol{m}_{\cdot i})\) keeps a lower bound that increases at every step; both converge at a rate set
        by the fraction of missing information, close to one when single cells are noisy, as when
        \(\boldsymbol{D}\to\boldsymbol{0}\) above
  \item majorise--minimise for the same model: with the scales fixed, \(\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p})\) is affine in \(\boldsymbol{p}\), so
        \(\log\det\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p})\) is concave \cite[p.~74]{boydConvexOptimization2004} and
        \(\boldsymbol{r}^{\top}\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p})^{-1}\boldsymbol{r}\), a matrix-fractional function of the affine pair
        \((\boldsymbol{r},\boldsymbol{S}_{W})\), is convex \cite[Example~3.4, p.~76]{boydConvexOptimization2004}; the log-likelihood
        \eqref{eq:sc-loglik} is thus the convex function \(-\tfrac12\log\det\boldsymbol{S}_{W}(\boldsymbol{p})\) plus a concave one;
        replacing the convex part by its tangent minorises the log-likelihood, so each step maximises a concave function
        on the closed simplex, the likelihood increases at every step, and an exact zero is reachable; both curvatures
        held on 200 random instances; the decomposition fails for population references, whose covariance is quadratic in
        \(\boldsymbol{p}\)
  \item priors on the composition: Dirichlet, concave in ILR coordinates for every \(\alpha_{j}>0\), or
        logistic-normal; either keeps the posterior proper where Wald intervals degenerate
  \item routes to the full posterior: Laplace approximation with the analytic Hessian, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo
        on the closed-form marginal likelihood, or stochastic variational inference
  \item open problems: none of these algorithms is implemented; EM stalls as \(\boldsymbol{D}\to\boldsymbol{0}\);
        plugging in \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\) ignores its uncertainty
        (\vignette{theory-decovart-bayesian-wiener.html}{Wiener-filter vignette})
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Alternative strategies for cell-type-specific covariances}
\label{sec:grn}

Besides the mean of each cell type, DeCovarT needs \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) or \(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}\).
Gene--gene dependence estimated from single cells remains an open problem. 
Specifically, inferring mechanistic and directed regulatory edges typically requires additional data modalities,
such as chromatin accessibility (ATAC-seq) or transcription-factor binding information (TF-seq)
information \cite{badia-i-mompelGeneRegulatoryNetwork2023,maizelsGeneRegulatoryNetworks2026}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item the fitted precision is undirected, while most regulatory interactions are directed
  \begin{itemize}
    \item orient edges by pairing observations with interventions
    \item or add chromatin accessibility or transcription-factor binding
          \cite{badia-i-mompelGeneRegulatoryNetwork2023,maizelsGeneRegulatoryNetworks2026}
  \end{itemize}
  \item benchmarks show that inference can fail in common regimes even with multi-omic inputs
        \cite{nourisaGeneRNIBLivingBenchmark2025}
  \item open question: whether a causal graph improves \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\), or a consistent covariance is enough
  \item current fits use the variability inside one single-cell biological replicate; separating it from the
        between-donor layer needs \(R\ge2\) replicates (Section~\ref{sec:generalized-linear-model})
  \item reference uncertainty without a prior: \((\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\})\) are estimates, and four frequentist routes
        propagate their error into \(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}\)
  \begin{itemize}
    \item a joint likelihood of the bulk and the reference: with Gaussian cells,
          \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}/C_{j})\) and \((C_{j}-1)\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\) is Wishart with \(C_{j}-1\)
          degrees of freedom and scale \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\), so the bulk and reference log-likelihoods can be maximised
          together, with a graphical-lasso penalty on the precisions; the Schur complement of the joint
          information then widens the intervals for \(\boldsymbol{p}\), the likelihood counterpart of the noisy-mean term of
          \emph{RNA-Sieve} \cite{erdmann-phamLikelihoodbasedDeconvolutionBulk2021}
    \item stacked estimating equations: the reference moment equations and the bulk score solved together, with
          a \gls{sandwich} over both, give the two-step correction
          \(I_{pp}^{-1}+I_{pp}^{-1}I_{p\eta}\boldsymbol{V}_{\eta}I_{\eta p}I_{pp}^{-1}\) of Appendix~\ref{app:alignment}, with \(\boldsymbol{\eta}\) the reference
          moments; \emph{MEAD} treats the noisy signature as an errors-in-variables term
          \cite{xieStatisticalInferenceCell2026}
    \item estimation from a bulk cohort: \emph{DECALS} regresses the outer products of the residuals on the squared
          proportions to estimate the type-level covariances, with a finite-sample correction for the
          plugged-in proportions \cite{caiStatisticalInferenceCelltype2024}
    \item resampling: \texttt{reference\_bootstrap\_decovart()} resamples donors or cells and refits, and the bias of
          the plug-in precision has a closed-form correction (Appendices~\ref{app:mle-boundary} and~\ref{app:mle-firth})
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Ensemble deconvolution: early, intermediate and late integration}
\label{sec:ensembles}

Several references, omics or algorithms can be combined at three levels: early integration merges the inputs before
the fit, intermediate integration couples them inside one model, and late integration combines the outputs
\cite{barbotPromisesLimitsMultimodal2026}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item evidence from the HADACA3 benchmark (pancreatic cancer, RNA and DNA methylation, 627 submitted pipelines)
        \cite{barbotPromisesLimitsMultimodal2026}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item late integration of per-omic estimates, with weights inversely proportional to each omic's reconstruction
          error, reached the best median aggregate score (0.71), against 0.64 for the best early integration (scaled
          concatenation) and 0.71 for DNA methylation alone; integration raised the best case, not the average
    \item the only ensemble of algorithms tested, picking robust regression or NNLS by global reconstruction error,
          did not improve consistently on robust regression alone; intermediate integration and ensembles of
          references were not evaluated
    \item every method used one averaged profile per cell type, so no reference variance or covariance entered any fit
  \end{itemize}
  \item early integration in DeCovarT: stacking references or omics as rows of \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\), with a block
        covariance for each type \cite{chenUnicoUnifiedModelCell2025}; this keeps the likelihood but needs a cross-omic
        covariance, which only matched multi-omic references provide
  \item intermediate integration: one likelihood over several references, with the reference index as a random
        effect; the between-donor layer of \eqref{eq:four-layer} is this model for \(R\ge2\) single-cell donors, and
        ensembles over references and algorithms \cite{caiRobustAccurateEstimation2022} are its late counterpart
  \item late integration: pooling the composition estimates of several references or algorithms; DeCovarT returns a
        Fisher information with each estimate, so inverse-variance weights on the ILR chart give a pooled composition
        and its interval, a likelihood-based version of HADACA3's error-based weights; correlated errors between
        algorithms fitted to the same bulk must be estimated, for instance by a parametric bootstrap
  \item[] \todo[inline]{Prototype inverse-variance pooling of DeCovarT fits over references on the ILR chart, and compare
        it with error-weighted averaging on the HADACA3 datasets.}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Roadmap of other extensions}
\label{sec:roadmap}

Each extension keeps one of the two generative models and changes one part of it; none is implemented
(\vignette{theory-decovart-extensions-roadmap.html}{roadmap vignette}).

\begin{itemize}
  \item composition structure: sample covariates in a composition model on the log-ratio chart
        \cite{fanMusic2CellTypeDeconvolution2022}
  \item reference completeness: one unknown component absorbing an unrepresented population
        \cite{taiBayiceBayesianHierarchicalModel2021,montierthDeconvolutionSparsecountRNA2025,racleSimultaneousEnumerationCancer2017}
  \item observation space: isoforms \cite{heilingEstimatingCellTypeComposition2023}, stacked multi-omics with a
        block covariance \cite{chenUnicoUnifiedModelCell2025,barbotPromisesLimitsMultimodal2026}, and spatial spots
        whose compositions depend on their neighbours \cite{cableRobustDecompositionCell2022a}
  \item time\label{sec:dynamic-inference}: at least three time points are needed to recover dynamic interactions
        between genes; without prior knowledge or perturbation data, temporal ordering is the main route to directed
        interactions and sparser, contextualised networks; time-resolved compositions can carry a random-walk prior on
        log-ratio coordinates \cite{kimLongitudinalProfilingLowAbundance2025}
\end{itemize}
